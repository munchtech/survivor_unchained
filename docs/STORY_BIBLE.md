# Story bible

The truth under Survivor Unchained, for the people who write it. What the
player sees in chapter one is the surface; this is what it rests on, what
each person is not saying, where the hints are planted, and where the next
two chapters go. Keep chapter-one content consistent with it; never let a
character say the answers below out loud before their chapter.

How people talk: `VOICES.md`. What the world remembers and how it is
written: `godot/README.md` and `godot/logic/World/`.

---

## 1. Premise and spine

**The world under the world.** Under Thornhollow lies the **Morrow**: a
thing vast, pale and segmented, older than the old empire, alive, and
bound. The old empire's Seventh Legion found it, could not kill it, and
chained it ("Here the Seventh Legion buried what it could not burn"). The
chain is seven links, each a sigil cut into a great heart of stone, and
each heart was given a keeper: the **Wardens**, made by the Order of the
Morning Light to stand on the seven links and kill anything that came
near. The Ford-Warden at the Low Ford was one.

**Ember is the Morrow's light.** It leaks up through the ground as stone
and dust. The Order burned it in its chapel lamps and called it the
morning ("the dark is only the part of the day that hasn't happened
yet" was never a metaphor: their morning was chained underneath them).
The Watch burned it in the ford lamps to keep the Wardens asleep. The Dig
cooks it into slurry. Everyone in the valley lives by it, and almost
nobody knows what it is.

**The Unchained.** When someone dies in the dark near ember, sometimes the
Morrow's light goes into them and they get up. They burn at night, when
the chain is weakest (the Legion's sigils drink sunlight; the dark is
when they starve), and at dawn the light drains back down into the
ground and leaves them ordinary. That is the ember the player grows each
night and loses each dawn. Each Unchained is a piece of the Morrow
walking about, a link coming loose. The title means both things: the
survivor is unchained from death; and through people like them, the
Morrow is coming unchained.

**The survivor.** A traveller (who they were is their background) who
drowned at the Low Ford the night the Warden woke, and got up. They do not
know they died. The town half suspects (Chid: "You were cold when they
brought you in... Then you weren't"), the old orders would know at a
glance, and one person arranged it.

**Why the lamps were lit.** The chain is failing. Ten years ago the
ground under **Ashford**, a mining town up the valley, gave way into the
Morrow's outer workings: the **Ashford Fall**. Half the town went into the
dark; the year after was the **fever year**, ember sickness in the wells.
Vonnra, the last of the binders' line (Ash-of-Morrow: the family that kept
the Legion's sigils), knows the chain cannot be re-forged by the living:
the Legion's dead locked the inner door from inside ("It was never locked
from the outside"), and only something of the Morrow's own light can walk
through them. She needs an Unchained. So last winter she had twelve lamp-
irons forged for the Low Ford, paid in the old empire's square coin, and
relit the lamps to wake the Warden, knowing it would drown travellers at
the crossing, and that one of them might rise. The survivor is her answer.

## 3. The people: public face and hidden motive

Every one of these is hinted in chapter one and answered later. None of
them says it.

- **Mother Rook.** *Face:* the innkeeper who knows everything. *Hidden:* Vonnra
  pays her to report who comes up the Low Ford road, and paid her double
  for the survivor. Her mother carried the Order's lamp up from the chapel
  the year the Order "left", and brought Chid bread. Rook is loyal to the
  town, not to Vonnra, and will turn when she learns what the money bought.
- **Captain Holloway.** *Face:* a tired, honest captain. *Hidden:* he was the
  Ashford garrison's quartermaster. When the Fall came the Watch command
  turned the relief wagons back, and Holloway signed for the boots and
  supplies as delivered, to cover his commander. The garrison held the cave
  mouths barefoot and died. Maeca is the last of it, and does not know whose
  hand signed. He also has, unanswered, a letter from the north asking
  about "the one from the ford".
- **Maeca Barefoot.** *Face:* a hunter with a theory. *Hidden:* the Pack saved
  her life after Ashford, which is why she has never hunted a wolf. She
  knows what red meant at Ashford and has never told Holloway that half
  the Kerchiefs are her old neighbours.
- **Old Wenna.** *Face:* a rude herbalist. *Hidden:* she nursed the fever year,
  knows ember sickness by smell, and made the blightward mask for it. She
  has suspected for a decade that the "fever" came out of the ground.
- **Tam.** A child who is right. No secret; his family's farm is where the
  Dig's first new tunnel will open in chapter two.
- **Brannoc.** *Face:* a smith of few words. *Hidden:* he forged the Low Ford's
  new lamp-irons last winter for a buyer who paid in square coin and never
  showed a face. Ten of twelve were collected. He suspects whose coin it
  was.
- **Harlan Coyle.** *Face:* the grieving uncle, the most sympathetic man in
  town. *Hidden:* the Coyle Company has supplied the Dig's blasting ember for
  two years, through Pell's books and the Kerchiefs' road. He put Jory on
  the road with six crates he knew were bound for the hole, and told him not
  to take them over ruts. His grief is real; so is his guilt.
- **Jory Coyle.** Did not know. Will find out.
- **Pell Varrow.** *Face:* the villain of the caravan plot. *Hidden:* a careful
  man who lost his sister in the Ashford Fall, has read the receipts, and
  is doing the wrong things for the right reason (and for profit, because
  he cannot help it). "I'm not a good man. I'm a careful one."
- **Rav Cutwell.** *Face:* the defector, the outcast's friend. *Hidden:* Redcowl
  is his brother, Dunstan. Rav still drinks with the toll clerk and still
  sends word down to the Roost: he gave Redcowl the night the Coyle
  wagons would come. The clerk's key he "found on a stool" he had taken
  himself. He is ashamed of all of it and will do it again.
- **Chid.** *Face:* the Fool, a priest of a dead order. *Hidden:* he is an
  Unchained of the Order's day, near two hundred years old, who cannot stay
  dead and does not age. He wrote the note in Ashe's trunk ("Keep the
  lights lit. — C.") to a friend. He has seen the dead get up before; he
  knows what the survivor is, and is the gentlest person who knows.
- **Vonnra Ash-of-Morrow.** *Face:* toll-keeper, far seer, the one who reads
  your fortune. *Hidden:* she relit the ford lamps to make an Unchained, and
  means to use the survivor to re-forge the chain: to walk them down through
  the Legion's dead and bind them in as the new link, forever. She believes
  it is the only way the valley lives. The toll clerk who sent Coyle's
  wagons off the road was her clerk; she let it happen, because a ruined
  Coyle sells no more ember to the Dig. She is the trusted figure with the
  deepest secret, and her reasons are good.
- **Dame Keegan Orme.** *Face:* comic, earnest, probationary. *Hidden:* she is
  the last of the Vigil, and does not know it. Chapter four of her handbook
  is "Of the Unchained, and Their Return to the Dark". She has started to
  see the signs in the survivor and is very much hoping she is wrong.
- **Sella.** *Face:* the blue room's frank, funny professional. *Hidden:* she
  sells what is said upstairs, and Vonnra outbids everyone for it. A lover
  with a motive, though not a cruel one: she would sell Vonnra a lie for
  you, if asked and paid.
- **Redcowl** (Dunstan Cutwell). *Face:* the bandit chief with cages. *Hidden:*
  the leader of Ashford's dispossessed, who kept his prisoners fed and
  who would, if anyone asked him straight, fight the Dig before the Watch
  would. The bad man who is the closest thing the valley has to a
  resistance.
- **Ysolde Marrow, the Wayfinder.** *Face:* a cartographer of arenas. *Hidden:*
  she writes down who comes back from her maps, and sells the list to "a
  gentleman in the north who likes silver ink": the Vigil's Lord-Exchequer,
  who is collecting Unchained. She has noticed the survivor comes back more
  often than most.
- **Grimtunnel.** *Face:* a thieving Boss. *Hidden:* a believer. He thinks he is
  freeing a god.
- **Snib.** Exactly what he looks like. Will survive everything.

## 6. Chapters two and three: twists, betrayals, reversals

### Chapter two: The North Road

The Dig breaks through under Tam's farm, and the Waystation feels what
Ashford felt. Keegan finally steps aside, or tries to stop you: by now she
has seen the signs, and chapter four says what she must do.

- **The Vigil's chapterhouse** is not dead but changed: cages of silver, the
  risen kept for a war in the south, a ledger with the survivor's name in
  it in Ysolde's hand. *Betrayal:* the Wayfinder sold you. *Reversal:* the
  Vigil, the order that "guarded" the valley, is now the threat.
- **Harlan exposed**, by Jory, or by Pell's receipts. The grieving uncle armed
  the Dig. *Choice:* hand him to Holloway, let him make it right (he funds
  the evacuation), or let the town find out its own way.
- **Pell returns** (if he fled) or is about to hang (if exposed) with the
  receipts that prove he was trying to stop the Dig. *Reversal:* the villain
  of chapter one is the one man with the numbers. Free him, or let the law
  that was right about the caravan be wrong about everything else.
- **Rav's tip-off** comes out; Redcowl (if alive) comes to the Waystation
  with forty-one mouths and the only people willing to fight in the
  tunnels. *Reversal:* the bandits are the army.
- **Holloway's letter.** The north wants the one from the ford delivered.
  Holloway has not answered it. *Choice for him, consequence for you:* he
  hands you over, or burns it (and Maeca learns who signed for the boots).
- **Sella** can feed Vonnra lies, if you ask and pay; Vonnra will know, in the
  end, and Sella pays for it.

### Chapter three: The Morrow

Down the stair behind the sealed door, through the Legion's dead, who let
the survivor pass because the survivor is the Morrow's own light.

- **Vonnra's truth.** She lit the lamps. She made you. She needs you to carry
  the hearts (the stolen one among them) back to the seven notches and lie
  down in the chain as its new link, forever. Her reason is the valley,
  and she is not wrong about what happens if the chain breaks.
- **Chid's truth.** He is Unchained. If the chain is re-forged tight, every
  Unchained goes back to the dark, him included. He has known all along
  and wants you to choose without thinking of him; he is the one person
  who never asked anything of you.
- **Grimtunnel** at the bottom, holding the Warden's heart up to something
  that is praying. He is not a villain either; he is a believer at the end
  of his faith.
- **What the Morrow prays for.** To be let die. The chain has kept it alive
  and harvested for two thousand years; ember is its pain.
- **The ending,** three ways, all defensible:
  1. *Re-forge the chain* (Vonnra): you become the link; the valley keeps its
     lamps and its ember; every other Unchained (Chid) goes dark.
  2. *Break it and let the Morrow die*: no more ember, anywhere. The lamps go
     out, the Unchained die properly (you too, at dawn), the dark is only
     dark again. A world without its light, and without its cage.
  3. *Take the light* (only if you have refused Vonnra and lied to the
     Vigil): carry the Morrow's light up yourself, the first Unchained who
     stays lit by day. A new god in the valley, and a new thing to fear.

## 7. Romance

All adults, all consenting; romance carries the intrigue.

- **Sella** (paid; open from the first night). *How:* "How much for the
  night?", fifteen gold, any survivor. *Reveals:* after `rook`, `sella.buyers`:
  she sells what is said upstairs, Vonnra above all. *Costs:* gold; what you

### Intimate scenes and the explicit slots

The game will have a setting for intimate scenes (show in full, or cut
away), offered in the settings and when such a scene begins. Content reads
it as the fact `settings.intimacy`: `"full"` shows the full scene; anything
else (or nothing) shows the cut-away. **The lead wires the setting; nothing
in the game writes the fact yet.** Every intimate scene is written in full
before and after, with a cut-away version of the moment itself, and an
explicit-variant slot keyed to `settings.intimacy == "full"` that holds a
placeholder for the owner's writer:

| Slot | Node | Who, where, tone |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `sella.night` | Sella and the survivor, the blue room at the top of Rook's stairs, by lamplight; warm, unhurried and funny, tenderness at the edges and quickly put away; ends with her asleep across you and the sun up. |
| 2 | `maeca.blind` | Maeca and the survivor, the Hunters' Blind in the Verge at night; wordless, wary, careful hands that become sure ones, frost outside, the Pack far off. |
| 3 | `sella.free_night` | Sella and the survivor, the blue room, not for money for the first time; slower and less sure than her working nights, the patter dropping away; a door she bolts herself. |

Each slot's text starts `[explicit scene:` so it can be found and tested
for; replace the whole placeholder with the scene.
