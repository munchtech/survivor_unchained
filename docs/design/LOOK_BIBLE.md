# The look bible: one game (H2, 3 November; art director, look-h2)

**The look in one line: a real world painted in big masses, lit by ember against moon.** Forms stay real (anatomy, architecture, proportion). Surfaces are painted: broad masses, one brush, no photographic noise. Light carries every color: ember and lantern amber against moon indigo by night, sun gold against violet shade by day. Everything on screen (the world, her, the foes, the HUD, the spells, the storybook) is lit or drawn by that same light. The storybook's plates are the key art for palette and value; the 3D is the same world in light instead of ink. This page replaces `docs/art/look_bible/` where they disagree (its budgets, page 11, still hold).

**The frame question for every restyle:** would this frame ship in a AAA game, and is it the same game as the other twelve? Re-shoot with `python tools/style/frames.py shoot --out <round>`, compare with `frames.py compare`, measure with `python tools/style/palette.py <round>`. Each rule's number is a self-check before the judge, never a substitute for the judge.

| # | Rule | Measured (palette.py, the world crop) | Carried by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Value first.** At thumbnail size every frame reads as three masses: dark, middle, and a lit pool. The brightest area is a source or what it lights (her ember, a lamp, a window, a fire, a spell), never bare ground. Night is dark but never murky: the pool and the source carry the picture. | night play: key (p95/p50) 2.0+, pool 0.35+, Lp50 0.15-0.30; day: key 1.6+, Lp50 0.40-0.60 | `AtmospherePreset` key, hemi and exposure (`logic/World/Atmospheres.cs`); lamp and fire lights; the arena's own preset |
| 2 | **Shadows keep color.** Night shade is moon indigo to teal (Oklab hue 220-290); day shade violet-blue (240-310); dawn lilac. Never slate gray, never brown-black. | shH in range, shC 0.025+, gray% 20 or less | `GradeSettings.PaintShadow`/`PaintShadowK` per preset (`--grade-try shadow=#..,shadowk=..` to trial) |
| 3 | **Two lights per scene, one warm.** Warm belongs to sources: her ember (#ff8a3a), lantern and window amber (#ffd07a), fire; dawn and day sun pale gold (#f3d9a0). The cold side is the sky and moon. A night frame's warmth comes from its sources only. | night: cold 60%+ of chroma, hiH 50-90 (the sources) | the presets' `KeyColor`, `HemiSky`, `Rim`; `Lamps`/`Campfire` colors; her `Ember` |
| 4 | **Vibrance, not saturation.** Dull midtones (mud, stone, cloth, bark) gain chroma; skin hues are held; brights stay rich, not bleached. | midC 0.045-0.07; clip under 1% | `GradeSettings.PaintVib`/`PaintHi`/`PaintVibDark`; `PaintShadowSkin = 1` wherever she is seen |
| 5 | **One brush.** Detail under about 2 cm in the world is painted out; what is left is flat patches following the form, low-contrast strokes along the grain, dark cavities and lit top edges. The stroke is one size in the world (about 6-10 cm, so 5-8 px at the play camera), whatever the asset's texel density. | (a judge at 1:1; the brush size by the asset's texels per meter) | `tools/assets/paint_tex.py` presets; to add: `--texels-per-m` scaling `ms` and the stroke, presets `ground`, `cloth`, `leather`, `iron`, `bronze`, `skin`, `hair` |
| 6 | **What painted means per material.** Stone: chunky planes, warm ochre-gray tops, deep cool joints. Ground and roads: two or three value masses (ruts, verge, puddle), no gravel noise. Foliage: clumps read as shapes, a light top, a cool core, no per-leaf sparkle. Cloth and leather: folds as soft planes, a sheen only on the lit edge. Metal (iron, steel, bronze): broad reflected bands of the key and sky colors, no fine scratches or speckle. Water: value bands of the sky, moon or sun reflections as soft strokes, no glossy plastic plane. Skin: her own hue and soft forms, pores and blotches gone at game cameras, warm terminator, cool shade. Hair: the clump shapes and their light, copper kept rich. | (a judge at 1:1) | paint_tex presets (above); `kit.gdshader` (foliage light), the water shader's `water_air`, her shaders |
| 7 | **Line and edge.** No outlines in 3D. Edges come from value and color; silhouettes always separate from the ground by value or a cold rim (the crowd at night). The painted cavity line is the 3D cousin of the storybook's ink. | (the crowd: a judge at the play camera) | paint_tex `cavity`/`edge`/`top`; `CrowdRim`, `CrowdFill` (`vat.gdshaderinc`) |
| 8 | **The HUD is an object in this light.** Bronze and glass, painted like the world's metal: broad bands, an ember-warm front and an indigo rim, no photographic micro-detail, a value under the play so the world reads first. Reading text on parchment cream (#e8dcc0) or ink cream (#e8dcc4); one gold line (#d9b56a). Marks in the world (telegraphs, pins, rings) are light on the ground in the palette, not vector lines or stripes. One screen language: the Lamps, the result and the book are the same bronze, iron, parchment and ink, not a fourth style. | (a judge: the HUD over play and the screens) | `src/Ui/Style.cs` (the swatches), `hud_vessel.gdshader`, the Dg* pieces; `Fx/Palette.cs` `Tele*` for marks |
| 9 | **Spells: one family per school.** The only fully saturated pixels at night are spells and her ember. Each school takes its Core, Glow and Dim from `Palette.Of(school)` and nothing else: fire is the ember's amber, frost the moon's blue, holy the dawn gold, shadow violet-indigo, storm pale blue-white, nature and arcane kept rarer. Shapes are painted (soft-edged tongues, strokes, motes), never hard geometry. | (a judge on the skill sheets) | `src/Fx/Palette.cs`; every effect shader reads its colors from it (no colors in shader code) |
| 10 | **The storybook is the same world drawn.** Its palette is this page's (indigo and teal night, ember gold, dawn gold, cream), its values the same three masses; the carved line is the only difference. The plates stay as they are; new plates keep the swatches. | sb plates: hues and swatches as palette.jpg's 02 strip | `tools/storybook/ink_recarve.py` quantize palette; `StoryArt.Compose` |
| 11 | **Her: forms untouched, surfaces the same.** Mesh, rig, face and proportions never change. Her skin, hair and cloth take the world's treatment: a paint pass over her existing maps (rule 5's skin, hair, cloth, leather and metal presets at her texel density), lit by the same two lights. Skin hue protected (`PaintShadowSkin = 1`), copper hair rich (rim at most 3, fire 0.75-0.9). She is painted after the world, to match it. | her skin hue h 15-90 unchanged by the grade (a check on the close-up) | `heroine_skin.gdshader`, `heroine_outfit.gdshaderinc`, `heroine_hair*.gdshader`; paint_tex presets; `Atmospheres.CloseNightPaint` |

**Off the bible today, in short** (the frames: `survivorsunchained_inputs\look_h2\r0\`; the list with fixes and costs: `docs/team/hook/H2_restyles.md`): the arena nights (09, 10, 12) are murky brown-black with gray shadows (shH 66-92, gray 43-62%, cold 0-3%) and pale smeared foes; every play frame is flat (key 1.2-1.8 against the storybook's 2.9 and C01's 3.2); the ground and stone are photographic noise; the HUD's bronze is the most realistic object on screen; the Lamps page is a navy constellation, a fourth language; the Warden's river is a glossy plane and his telegraphs neon vector lines.
