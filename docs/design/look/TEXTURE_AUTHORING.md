# B3 texture authoring: painted-block maps from form maps (L1, round 1, 4 Nov)

**What it is FROM** (PIPELINE.md rule 4): (a) the game's need: the play camera (the brush 5-8 px), the close-up; (b) the bible's rules 5, 6, 9, 10 and the boards MB2-MB4 (through the material library); (c) inherited: nothing. The painter never reads today's textures; an output is never hand-fixed (a fault goes back into the painter or its inputs).

## The stage's contract
| | |
|---|---|
| **Source** | the asset's mesh (its own forms) + its class per block; `godot/data/look/materials.json` (each class's `paint`: `albedo` (B2 round 2: the boards' light divided out), `brush_cm`, `spread_L`, `edge`, `cavity`; `cel.spec` for roughness); the bible's rule 9 numbers (in `paint_core.py`'s header) |
| **Build** | 1. `blender -b -P tools/look/bake_forms.py -- prop MESH OUT` (or `setts OUT` for a laid-stone ground tile): one deterministic bake (fixed seed and samples, no AA on IDs, one unwrap at one texel density, 256 px/m asked, nearest power of two) into `forms.npz`: mask, block ID, class ID, world position, world normal, AO (0.35 m), cavity (AO at 3 cm), convexity (inside-AO at 3 cm), thickness (inside-AO at 0.4 m), height (from position), and the re-unwrapped `mesh.glb` (one material per class, `<asset>_<class>`, so the library's assign rules give the class). 2. `python tools/look/paint_core.py FORMS OUT` writes `albedo.png` (blocks with the light painted in), `normal.png` (big forms only), `rough.png` (by class), `paint.json` (its numbers and the core's version). |
| **Check** | `paint.json`'s numbers against rule 9: each block's interior spread within its class's `spread_L` (±0.06 Oklab L), the brush at the class's world scale (6-10 cm, so 5-8 px at play), the share of detail under 3 cm, VRAM; the core's `VERSION` on every output (a stale map fails the check; round 2 adds `paint_core.py --check DIR` and the game-side "maps on the model" test). |
| **Game output** | the three maps per asset (or per ground tile, world-triplanar), the class's cel preset applied by B2's loader by the material's name |

**Blocks come from the forms:** a block is a part of the source (its loose part, welded back where the exporter split it, parted at creases over 80°); a rock's single shell is split into a few big planes by normal (38°). A tile's blocks are its laid stones. On her (below), a block is P1's paintable region. **The light painted in:** a top-lit gradient (facing up and high on the asset; 40% of the spread), the brush (60%: flat dabs of a 3D cell field in the block's own frame, long along its first axis, at `brush_cm`, softened at 1.2 cm so nothing sits under 3 cm; seamless across UV seams, being 3D), a lit bevel stroke (`edge.dL`, at least `edge.width_cm`) on convex creases and convex joints between blocks, a cool cavity (`cavity.dL`, its hue and chroma) in the 3 cm cavity and the big AO. **Normal:** a tile's stones baked onto the tile; a prop's each block a 4 cm rounded edge (1.5 cm high) from the 3D distance to the next block; nothing smaller. **Roughness:** 1 for every class with no highlight (rule 6), 0.45 for the highlit ones (metals, gloss).

## Character-side and world-side, one core
- **The core** lives once, in the game repo: `tools/look/paint_core.py` (and its contract `forms.npz`). Neither side copies it: Marionette calls the repo's file by path (its settings already hold the game dir, `toGameDir`), and every output records the core's `VERSION`, which the checks on both sides compare to the current one; a change to the painting bumps `VERSION`, so a stale map anywhere fails its check instead of drifting.
- **Character-side (a Marionette stage):** her skin and gear, then NPCs. A forge job (`/api/forge/paint`, like `/api/forge/smartmesh`) with inputs (the mesh from the body or gear stage, P1's region ID map from her UV layout, the classes from `her_paint_regions.json` and `materials.json`) and a check, no hand steps. The bake half is Marionette's planned bakes stage ("the bakes" in PIPELINE.md), which writes the same `forms.npz`; it replaces `bake_forms.py`'s loose-part blocks with the region ID map. `her_paint_regions.json` v1 holds as it is: its regions are blocks, its warm and cool zones the painter's zone layers; what P1 delivers with her UVs is a region ID map and a zone map (the zones placed by her landmarks), which is P1's layout, not a change to the list, so no v2. Her flat face albedo stays R10's or L1's call.
- **World-side (this repo):** kit props, buildings, ground tiles (setts, road, soil), rocks, foliage cards. `bake_forms.py` + `paint_core.py` run as one build per asset class (round 2: a `tools/look/build.py` over a list in data, its outputs under `godot/art/world/painted/`, and the kit builders' meshes taken as the source). The arena floor and the square are remade through it in Stage C.
- **FX authoring stays in this repo** (B6's language), outside the painter.

**The arms picked:** A procedural (the own painter); B AI-assisted (Z-Image + ControlNet on the form maps, from A's albedo, re-quantized: only its in-block value kept, as the brush); C, earned because it costs nothing more (the same AI paint), the AI's own in-block light kept too (clamped to the spread, a little of its hue). Block colors, the bevel, the cavity and the normal are the same in every arm, so they differ only in where the brush and in-block light come from. The bake-off: `tools/look/bakeoff.py` (sheets under `survivorsunchained_inputs\look_l1\b3\bakeoff\`); the judge picks; round 2 builds the winner into the pipeline.

## Round 2 (5 Nov): the winner built in
**The bake-off's verdict:** A, the procedural painter (the art director: at 1:1 the arms barely differ; B and C put 0.30-0.35 of the setts' detail under 3 cm against rule 9; 4-8x the build time and the GPU; the AI painted barrels into the barrel's own UV). The AI arms are closed; they come back only if a Stage C or D judge finds the close-up surfaces lack brush character.

**Albedo v2, through the game's own rig** (`tools/look/calib.py`; `paint.albedo.v` 2, its `method` beside it, `paint.lit` kept): round 1's albedo divided each board by a light read off its "gray" setts and plaster, which the boards paint warm, so classes came out lilac and pale. Now each class's swatch (the lookdev swatch row, drawn as the painted maps are: metallic 0, the painter's roughness) is shot at albedo 1 under the day and night rigs (the stage's fire off) at the play camera and a close-view camera (`--b3-cams swatches`: the close view's bearing, 35 degrees of pitch, the world's light being the rig's whatever the lens); its lit and shade faces give the class's response per rig and camera; albedo = the boards' day samples over that response, per channel, least squares over the samples; iterated three times on the swatches redrawn in the solved colors (the bands, grade and clipping are not linear). Clamps: reflectance 0.008-0.9; rule 4 (a town surface's relit day lit side at C 0.07 or the board's own, if higher; C 0.14 on natural classes). **Relit check** (`calib.py check`): day 23/23 classes within Oklab 0.04 of the boards' lit side at both cameras (worst satin 0.034, hair 0.029); night 0/10 against the boards' moonlit samples (`lit_night` boxes on MB3/MB4's night rows): stone +0.18 L too light, foliage, water and grass 0.11-0.18 too dark. One albedo answers the day; the night's per-hue response is the night rig and grade's (B4), not a color's. Found on the way: the swatch row was drawn inside out (wound counter-clockwise), each box showing its inner floor in its own shadow; fixed in `GameLookdev.Swatch.cs`.

**The world-side build** (one command per asset, deterministic: `--twice` compares two runs byte for byte):
`python tools/look/build.py barrel|boulder|setts|all [--twice]` (the list: `godot/data/look/painted.json`) bakes the forms (Blender turn), paints (arm A, `paint.albedo`), checks, and writes `godot/art/world/painted/<asset>/` (albedo, normal, rough, paint.json, mesh.glb) and `painted/maps.json`, installed in game by `--b3-painted` (B3Maps.cs; off by default until the art is judged). **The check** (`paint_core.py --check DIR`, `VERSION` 2): the version and the classes' inputs hash on paint.json and in every PNG (a map painted from an older albedo fails); each block's spread within ±0.06 L; the brush 6-10 cm (and its px at play, 73 px/m: 5-8 wanted); detail under 3 cm at most **0.15** (A's soft dab edges give 0.03-0.14; the AI arms' 0.30-0.35 grain is what read as sub-3 cm detail at 1:1 at the close view, about 9 px per cm); each block's middle color inside its class's range.

**The setts' layout** is the class's (`paint.layout`: courses 0.34-0.46 m, stones 1.5-2.0 courses long), measured from target 13 (the setts beside the woman in purple against her height) and MB3's shapes; `bake_forms.py setts` reads it, so Stage C's square remake uses it.

## The forge job (character side): the spec Marionette wires in one step
- **Endpoint:** `POST /api/forge/paint` (like `/api/forge/smartmesh`), body `{mesh, regions, out}`; no hand steps.
- **Inputs:** the body or gear stage's mesh (GLB, her UV layout); P1's region ID map (PNG, one 8-bit ID per paintable region of `docs/design/look/her_paint_regions.json` v1, in her UVs) and its zone map; the game repo dir (`toGameDir`), from which it reads `godot/data/look/materials.json` and calls `tools/look/paint_core.py` by path (never a copy).
- **Command:** (1) the bakes stage writes `OUT/forms.npz` + `forms.json` in `bake_forms.py`'s contract (mask, blk = the region ID, cls = the region's class index into `forms.json.classes` from `her_paint_regions.json`, pos, nrm, ao, cavity, convex, thick; `kind` "char", `ppm`, `asset`); (2) `python <game>/tools/look/paint_core.py OUT OUT/paint --arm A`; (3) `python <game>/tools/look/paint_core.py --check OUT/paint` (exit 0 = pass).
- **Check:** the core's check as above (version and inputs hash, spread, brush, detail, colors in range); the job fails on a non-zero exit and shows the lines.
- **Outputs:** `OUT/paint/albedo.png`, `normal.png`, `rough.png`, `paint.json` (all carrying `VERSION` and the inputs hash), handed to the view and export stages.
- Not yet run on a character mesh this round (no Marionette edit; the stand-in run is the next step: her mesh with a region map rasterized from `her_paint_regions.json` v1).
