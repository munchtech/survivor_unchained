# SB00 · Stay Where They Reach

**The storybook lore intro.** Plays before C01's wake on a new journey · about 89 s · skippable · drawn (the storybook style: painted, inked plates in layers, slow camera moves, parallax, living touches, cut and scored like film; OWNER_NOTES 6 October)

> The owner, 18 October: "before the opening cinematic there is a storybook drawn cinematic explaining what we believe to be true about ember (obviously writing will have some twists and turns) ... have it end with blublublub drowning sounds fade to black - into our cinematic wakeup. you know the style some games have 'long ago bla bla did bla bla and this is bla bla, waiting for a hero to bla bla'".

## What it does

The valley's tale of ember, as the Watch and the common folk tell it, in eight short lines. Something old was chained under the hills. Its light seeps up as ember, which the valley burns and sells. In the dark the dead get up full of it, and putting them down spills it out. At first light it goes back into the ground. Keepers and lamps held the crossings, and somebody has lit the lamps at the Low Ford with ember again. The last line is the rule every child here learns: stay in the light. Then a traveler on the ford road walks out of the firelight to fill a flask at the water. Something brings a blue lamp close over the water to the traveler's face, and the tale ends under the water, in black, in C01's wake.

- **The player comes away knowing:** what ember is (light from under the ground); where it is found (in the dark, in the dead, spilling out when they fall); that it lasts only until first light; that the Low Ford's lamps are wrong this year; and that a traveler drowned there tonight.
- **"Waiting for a hero", turned:** the tale waits for nobody. The drowned stand along the road, waiting. The camera finds a traveler who has heard none of it, and the tale's last rule is the one the traveler breaks. A prophecy of one who comes back would hand the player Act 2's answer (*what you are*) in the first minute.
- **Every claim is a belief, and every belief is a half-truth.** The story spends three acts turning them; see "The half-truths".

## Who tells it

**An old Watchman.** Cast in the dead Watchman's voice (`watchman`: 60, northern English, VO_CAST), the man the player finds sitting dead against the Watch-post an hour later. He is Corran, though nobody names him here. The town wrote him down as a deserter, but he died at his post. His belt-book's last lines ("Lamps at the Low Ford lit again, and not by us... I can hear it singing in the water") are echoed by his tale's last lines, and the devout survivor hears this voice again from the body ("It broke its own lamps, coming for me. Twice."). He tells only the common tale, nothing of his own, so he stays "only what is said of" him (bible §5).

**Not the narrator.** She speaks in the present tense and the second person, says what can be seen and never what it means, and is cast "no warmth, no winter's tale" (VOICES.md, the owner's 6 October casting). A lore tale would break all of that in its first minute. It would also cost Act 3 its turn, because her plainness is the mother holding back. Her first words stay the game's first narrated words: "Your bedroll has not been slept in." She is silent here, as she is at the water's edge in the prologue and through Rook's confession. **Nobody speaks at the water's edge.**

**Performance.** A man telling a new Watchman what he was told when he was new, at the post, at dusk, with the lamp just lit. Plain, low, unhurried and tired, with a soldier's flatness. He is not a storyteller making music, not a trailer voice, and has no relish for the horror. Contractions; small breaths. "Not them." is flat, the way you say a thing you have decided to believe. "No more than that." is a touch quicker, said by a man who would rather not look at it. "Nobody remembers why." is a shrug. "Not us." has an edge, the Watch's pride. "And something in the water is singing." drops lower. He says "Lamps are lit. Stay where they reach." the way he has said it every dusk of his life, not as a warning; the picture makes it one. Record close and dry, in a small stone room's air with no tail. About 2.0 words a second with his pauses.

## Trigger and facts

- Plays on a new journey when creation is confirmed (`GameFront.NewJourney`), before C01, and once a journey, like C01.
- Reads nothing: every plate is the same for every survivor. Sets a profile flag, `seen.sb00`, because a journey's facts don't carry over, so later journeys may skip it at once.
- Until the storybook format is built, nothing waits on it: C01 plays as written.

## The plates

Times are from the first frame of black. Each plate is one painting in layers, and the move is the camera's across those layers. Plates change by ink: the next plate's ink spreads up through the last.

### P0 · The lamp at the post (0:00-0:02.5)
- **Drawn:** black, then an old man's hand at a stone window: gray hairs, scarred knuckles, a slate blue-gray cuff (the Watch's cloth). The hand lights a small lamp, which catches warm yellow.
- **Move:** none. The lamp's light spreads across the page as ink and opens P1.
- **Sound:** wind over hills, the river far off, a striker, the flame catching. No music yet.

### P1 · Long ago (0:02.5-0:11)
- **Drawn:** two thousand years ago, a hillside at night. The Seventh Legion's column comes down with torches into a great cut in the hill, small figures in rust-red cloaks and dulled bronze under a standard marked VII. At the bottom of the cut lies one curve of something pale and ribbed, bigger than the cut, coming out of the earth the way a sleeping whale's back breaks the sea. Only that fragment: no face, no eyes, never the whole.
- **Move:** a slow descent from the column down the cut to the pale curve. Torches in the foreground, the column in the middle, the curve far below.
- **Living touches:** torch smoke; sparks falling into the dark. Once, over the plate, the pale surface lifts very slightly, like a sleeper's breath.
- **Line L1:** "Long ago, the Seventh Legion found something under these hills that wouldn't die,"
- **Sound:** the drone rises from nothing (the `Night` mood's root, as C01's shot 9). The solo instrument enters with the motif's first phrase (see Sound). Boots and armor far off, and one deep, slow sound from below, felt more than heard.

### P2 · The chain (0:11-0:15.5)
- **Drawn:** a chain whose links stand as tall as a man runs down into the dark. At the cut's lip the Legion's smiths close a link on an anvil. At the bottom of the cut the chain runs in through a great stone door, and the door is closing on it. Nobody goes in. Letters in an old hand are carved over it; nobody can read them at this speed (the vault's "Here the Seventh Legion buried what it could not burn").
- **Move:** down the chain, from the anvil to the door.
- **Living touches:** sparks from the hammer; the door's slow close. The pale glow inside narrows to a line, then goes out.
- **Line L2:** "and chained it there, in the dark."
- **Sound:** the hammer on the beat; the door's close lands as one low hit that ends the phrase.

### P3 · Ember (0:15.5-0:25.5)
- **Drawn:** a storybook cross-section. Deep down, dark earth, and below everything the faintest pale glow, seeping up past the door anyway. Threads of light rise slowly up through rock, roots and gravel, like sparks rising through water, and gather near the surface as small warm stones and dust in soil and riverbed. Above ground is the valley today: hills, a river, the Waystation's roofs where three roads meet, ember lamps burning gold and steady in its windows, a hearth, a hand passing coins for a glowing stone, a cart of stones on the road.
- **Move:** a slow rise up through the ground to the lit town.
- **Living touches:** the rising motes; chimney smoke; the hearth's steady flame.
- **Line L3:** "Its light still seeps up through the ground. We call it ember. It burns. It sells. The valley lives by it."
- **Sound:** a faint crystalline hiss rises with the motes. The town's sounds come in small and warm: a door, coins, a cart. The motif warms, and a second instrument plucks under it.

### P4 · The dead get up (0:25.5-0:37)
- **Drawn:** night, frost, a field by a road, with ember glinting in the soil like coals in ash. The frost cracks in a line (C01's crack), and a dead hand comes up. A body stands, slack and blank-faced, its light showing at the mouth, the eye sockets and the wounds like a lamp behind paper. A Watchman in slate blue-gray, with a round shield, puts it down with his spear. The light pours out of it onto the frost as a scatter of small glowing stones, and the body falls empty and dark. His breath smokes in the cold; theirs never does.
- **Move:** a slow drift left to right along the frieze: rising, standing, put down, spilling.
- **Living touches:** the frost cracking; the light pouring and settling; his breath.
- **Line L4:** "Where it lies thick, the dead get up after dark. Not them. Just the bodies, full of its light. Put one down and it spills out."
- **Sound:** frost splitting; the risen's rasp, breath with no breath in it (C01's); the spear; the spill as a soft bright scatter, the same family as the in-game ember pickup's sound. The motif turns down and the strings scrape.

### P5 · First light (0:37-0:46)
- **Drawn:** the same field at dawn: gray, then gold along the hills. The light drains out of the risen and down into the ground like water into sand, and they fold onto the frost as plain dead. The scattered stones dim and sink. The Watchman leans on his spear with his small lamp guttering, and his breath smokes.
- **Move:** a slow tilt from the draining ground up to the sun's edge.
- **Living touches:** the light sinking; breath; the first birds.
- **Line L5:** "At first light it sinks into the ground and takes back what it lent. No more than that."
- **Sound:** one sustained note, warm as the dawn. A single bird, then two.

### P6 · Keepers and lamps (0:46-0:56)
- **Drawn:** two ages in one plate, read left to right. **Left, the old priests:** a river crossing; priests in undyed pale linen on the bank lifting lamps that burn gold; in midstream, a lamp held high and clear of the river, whatever holds it lost in mist. Beyond, the river winds away past more crossings, each with its own small high light. **Right, the Watch, years on:** the same kind of crossing. A Watchman on a ladder fills a lamp on its post from an oil jug, and the flame is yellower and smoky. The water at the crossing is dark and smooth, with nothing over it.
- **Move:** a slow pan from left to right. The priests' gold ages in the ink into the Watch's yellow.
- **Living touches:** lamp flames; the oil pouring; smoke; the mist.
- **Line L6:** "The old priests set keepers at the crossings. After them, the Watch kept the lamps lit. Oil, never ember. Nobody remembers why."
- **Sound:** the motif in a plain, hymn-like setting for the priests; oil glugging into a lamp; the river.

### P7 · Last winter (0:56-1:03.5)
- **Drawn:** the Low Ford at night in winter, with snow on the banks and ice at the edges. Three new iron cages on posts, black and unrusted, burn **cold blue**. In midstream a fourth blue light hangs just clear of the water, its holder lost in its own glare. Nobody is in the picture.
- **Move:** a slow push toward the fourth light.
- **Living touches:** the blue flames; rings spreading under the fourth light.
- **Line L7:** "Last winter, somebody lit the lamps at the Low Ford again. Not us. With ember."
- **Sound:** the blue flames hiss, cold, unlike the warm fire's crackle. The music drops to the drone, with one high, cold harmonic.

### P8 · Waiting (1:03.5-1:11)
- **Drawn:** the ford road at night, now. A wagon lies on its side. The ditch beside it is full of the drowned, standing river-wet with weed in their hair, every face turned to the road (the prologue's ambush, waiting to come true). Beyond them, through the trees, the ford's three blue lamps burn low. Under the lamps, rings spread on the river where nothing broke the water.
- **Move:** a drift along the ditch, past the standing dead, to the rings under the lamps.
- **Living touches:** water dripping from the dead; weed stirring; the rings.
- **Line L8:** "Now the drowned stand along the ford road, waiting. And something in the water is singing."
- **Sound:** the music thins to almost nothing. Under the river, faint and far down, a deep voice is singing, too far down to make out the words: the motif, passed from the instrument to the river.

### P9 · The traveler (1:11-1:18)
- **Drawn:** dusk, the last light, the sky deep blue over a dull rose band. A small camp sits beside the ford road: a fire burning high, a log, a bedroll unrolled with its blanket still folded on it, a pack. A lone traveler sits by the fire, hood up, back to us (the title screen's stranger, drawn), a flask on a cord already wound round one wrist, reading a folded letter. Its lines of handwriting are too small to read. Far up the road to the north are the Waystation's lamps, small, and one lamp burning higher than the rest. Low through the trees by the water are three points of blue.
- **Move:** static wide. On the line's last word, the traveler folds the letter into the coat, stands, and walks toward the water. The figure crosses the edge of the firelight's warm ring into blue, and the camera follows at a walk.
- **Line L9:** "Lamps are lit. Stay where they reach."
- **Sound:** the fire crackling; one night bird. The solo instrument plays the motif's first phrase once, small and warm, and **stops as the traveler steps out of the light.** From here there is no score.

### P10 · The water (1:18-1:27.5, then black to 1:29.5)
Wordless. **The narrator and the Watchman are both silent here; nobody speaks at the water's edge.**
- **10a (3.5 s):** the river at the last of dusk, smooth as a mirror. The traveler kneels on the flat stones at the edge, three-quarters from behind with the hood up, and holds the flask under. A little upstream, the three blue lamps burn on their posts.
- **10b (2.5 s):** close on the water's surface: the traveler's reflection, the face lost in hood and ripple. The surface trembles. Blue light slides into the reflection from the river side and grows toward the reflected face until it erases it: a lamp brought close above the water, held clear of it by something its glare hides. The lamp never touches the river.
- **10c (3.5 s):** the hooded head lifts, seen from behind and to the side, and blue light washes over the edge of the hood. The lamp hangs a hand's breadth above the water, level with the hood. The flask is still in hand. Then the picture goes under: the camera tips into the water, into a drawn layer of black-green with bubbles rushing up and the blue lamp warped above the surface. **It fades to black over 2 s.**
- **Sound:** the river; the flask filling, a glug and a run of bubbles; the song coming up close from under the water (`cin_none_cross.call`, reused: *"(sung, under the water) Lamps are lit... stay where they reach..."*). A heavy pull and splash, then everything goes under: the roar drops to a muffle; bubbles burst, blub-blub-blub (the owner's word); cloth and a muffled thrash, with no voice, because the survivor is never heard in a cinematic. The song is very close now, inside the water. A heartbeat slows under it all. In the 2 s of black, every sound sinks away, further and further, as if down a well. The mix is gone before you can tell whether the heartbeat stopped. Silence.

## Sound and music

- **The motif is the Order's evening call:** the melody the Warden sings under the water (`cin_none_cross.call`). Write it once. The score carries it on one solo instrument, a bowed string with some grain in it, over the `Night` drone: from P1, warmer in P3, broken in P4, hymn-like in P6, and small and warm by the fire in P9. In P8 it passes to the river, and P10 sings it. The player hears the valley's safety become the lure, and hears it again in C02 when the thing in the ford sings it.
- **The score ends when the traveler leaves the firelight.** The water and the drowning have no music, and C01 has none until its shot 9.
- **Mix:** the Watchman close and dry, every plate's sounds under him. P10 is the loudest and closest moment, and the black after it is the quietest.

## For the illustrator

- **The traveler is anyone:** hood up, back or three-quarter back, gloved hands. No face, no hair, no body shape that reads one sex, no calling's weapon. Pack, bedroll, flask, letter. This is the title screen's rule.
- **Ember's colors:** warm orange-gold in the ground, in the dead, in hearths and open lamps, the color of the stones the player gathers (UI `Ember` #ff8a3a, highlight #ffd07a). **Blue only in an iron cage** (the ford's lamps, #8ac8ff). An oil flame is yellower and smoky. The color is the story: the tale's lamps are warm, and the Low Ford's are wrong.
- **The Watch** wears slate blue-gray (#4a5064), carries round shields and spears, and its old men have gray beards. **The Legion** is rust-red and dulled bronze with Latin letters, never shiny. **The priests** wear undyed pale linen.
- **The thing under the hills** is only ever one pale ribbed curve. **The keeper** is only ever a lamp held clear of the water, its holder unseen (P6, P7, P10). C02 shows what holds it.
- **Breath:** the living's smokes in the cold (P4, P5); the dead's never does. C01's shot 8 pays it.
- **Continuity with C01:** the flask on a cord round the wrist; the bedroll unrolled with its blanket folded; the letter dry here and run to blue water in C01; the high lamp far up the road.
- The drawings are ours and carry the valley's soul, not generic fantasy and no AI look. Any generated art follows the provenance rules (ours, local, logged).

## What it must never do

Name or show the mother, the twenty-six, Vonnra, the tower, Chid, or anyone wading or on the far bank. Name or describe the Unchained, or say why anyone can burn ember. Show anyone going in through the Legion's door (C13's "It was never locked from the outside"). Say "the Morrow", or put "gone to the Morrow" anywhere near "its light". Use the narrator's voice. Show the Warden, or put his lamp in the water. Each of these spends a reveal the story keeps for later.
