# Sample scenes: the treatment in its own words

Drafts of six beats from `TREATMENT.md`, written as they would play, so the
owner can judge the writing as well as the plan. Nothing here is in the
game's data. Conventions are the data's: a parenthesis in lower case is how a
line is said; a capitalised parenthesis is the narrator's; italic lines are
the narrator's; `>` is a choice.

---

## 1. The first dawn (C04, shot A7)

*The sun clears the trees, and the ember goes back into the ground.*

(Her breath smokes. She breathes again, to see it. She drinks the river.)

*You try to call up your mother's face, and find it is not quite where you
left it.*

*Her voice is still there. "Lamp's lit, Wick. Stay where it reaches."*

(Three seconds of nothing. She walks up the road toward the gate.)

> **Performance.** No weight on any of it. The quoted line is said exactly
> as plainly as the rest: the narrator does not do a voice for the mother.
> (Under twist A it is her own voice saying her own words, and nobody will
> hear that the first time.)

---

## 2. The first night's return (the south gate, dawn)

*The gate has stood open all night. Captain Holloway is asleep on a stool
inside it, his lamp burned out and a bottle by his boot. Maeca sits on the
step beside him with her crossbow across her knees. She is awake.*

**Maeca:** (a nod at him) Waits up for you. (a beat) Somebody waits up for
him.

**Holloway:** (awake all at once, the way soldiers wake, and finding you)
...One in. (He looks past you at the empty road.) Count's right.

> "You waited all night?"

**Holloway:** Gate was open. Somebody had to stand in it.

> "Thank you, Captain."

**Holloway:** Don't. I'll want paying.

(He gets up, and puts his shoulder to the gate, and bars it. Maeca picks up
his bottle, looks at what's left in it, and pours it out on the step.)

**Maeca:** That's your last.

## 3. Brannoc: the pride, then the question

**His first meeting, as she turns to go:**

**Brannoc:** Came up the Low Ford road? (The hammer.) Lit that. Ten irons.
(The hammer.) Best I've done.

**His barks at the forge, days 1 and 2:**
- "Girl's first new boots. Irons paid for 'em."
- "Told her: walk where it's lit."

**C07, on the truth, after "Got up, and you put her down":**

**Brannoc:** (His eyes go to the iron at your belt, and stay there.)
...Where'd you get that.

> "Out of the fist of the thing in the ford."

**Brannoc:** (He holds out his hand. You put the iron in it. He turns it to
the forge, and his thumb goes under the socket and finds the mark without
looking.) Lifts it up. (a breath) To see your face. ...Does it.

> "It does."

**Brannoc:** (The forge ticks. He does not move for a long time.) Knew my
mark before she knew her letters.

(Longer.)

**Brannoc:** She'd have thought I'd come for her.

(Nothing. The bar on the anvil goes from orange to grey. Then, because he
needs one mercy from somebody:)

**Brannoc:** ...Was it quick?

> "It was quick." / "It wasn't."

**Brannoc:** (quick) (A long time.) ...Thank you. (He puts the iron down on
the anvil, very carefully, next to the cooling bar.) Forge is shut.

**Brannoc:** (not quick) (He nods, once, as if you've told him a price.)
Forge is shut.

## 4. Holloway's confession (the gate, the night of the last tremor)

*The ground turns over under the square, and every lamp on the wall dips and
comes back. At the gate Holloway hasn't moved. He's listening to something.*

**Holloway:** Hear that.

> "Hear what?"

**Holloway:** Knocking. (He listens.) From under. (He drinks.) No. Course
not.

> "Captain. What happened at Ashford?"

**Holloway:** (He counts on his fingers, loses his place, and starts again.)
Ground went. Lower town with it. Night. Half the garrison on the hill, at
the cave mouths. Half down the shaft. Ladders. (He drinks.) Me at the top.
Lamp. Windlass. Lid. Counting them up. One. Two. (A long time.) Ninety-one.
Then I looked down.

> "What did you see?"

**Holloway:** Dead. Climbing. Under the last of ours, close as that. (He
holds one hand flat over the other.) Four hundred behind me, asleep. Bairns.
(He drinks.) Lid down. Bar across. Sat on it. (A laugh, with nothing in it.)
Three days, they knocked.

> "You saved the upper town."

**Holloway:** Both numbers are right. That's the trouble with counting.

> "You shut them in."

**Holloway:** I did.

> "Did anyone come out from under it?"

**Holloway:** No. (He looks at the bottle.) No.

> (Say nothing.)

**Holloway:** (He counts. You can see his lips do it.)

**Then, whichever she chose:**

**Holloway:** Wrote them down at the cave mouths. With the others. Widows
get paid for the cave mouths. (He finds the cup's empty, and keeps holding
it.) Go to bed. (He gets up, holding the gatepost, and looks down the dark
road.) Somebody's still out. ...Always somebody still out.

> **Performance.** Drunk, he talks like his own ledger: entries, no
> subjects, numbers without nouns. He never raises his voice and never
> cries. "Bairns" is the only soft word, and he hurries past it.

## 5. The rope (C20, the Penhale farm, first light)

(Everyone is up but him. Tam's Pa is on his back in the mud, alive, and
furious.)

**Tam's Pa:** (down the hole) And you took your bloody time, CAPTAIN!

(From below, a laugh comes up the hole. It surprises him as much as anyone.)

**Holloway:** (below) Ha!

(He climbs, hand over hand, the lamp hooked at his belt. Maeca is at the
post where the rope is tied. The survivor is beside her. His face comes up
into the light at the lip, under them. He looks up at Maeca, and grins.)

**Holloway:** Count's right.

(One stroke of her knife at the post. The rope runs. He does not cry out.
The lamp at his belt goes down the dark, very small, and goes out.)

(No music. She puts the knife away.)

**Maeca:** (flat, to nobody) Ninety-two.

(Only then:)

> "Maeca." / (Take hold of her.) / (Say nothing.)

**Maeca:** (if she is the survivor's lover, to "Maeca.") You were up.

## 6. The bottom of the stair (C43, beat 5)

(The names: *Aldo*, in a woman's voice, a long way off. *Ewan*, in a boy's.
*Corran*. *Dannet*, over and over. Close by, once, a girl: *Da*. Then, out
of all of it, from below, in the voice that has told her every night since
the ford what was in front of her:)

**Her mother:** Lamp's lit, Wick.

> "Mam?" / (Listen.) / "Let them go."

**Her mother:** There you are. (a breath) I came up with you. Somebody had to
tell you where you were.

> "Why didn't you say?"

**Her mother:** You'd have sat down in the road and waited for me.

*Her face is exactly where you left it.*

> **Performance.** The narrator's own voice and pace, unchanged, for the
> first time coming from a place (the sound moves out of the air and into
> the pale thing below). "There you are" is the only line in the game she
> lets any warmth into. The last line is the narrator again, describing her
> own face, and it is said as plainly as "Your bedroll has not been slept
> in."
