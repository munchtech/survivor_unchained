# Counters: the matrix

What answers what. Each section crosses the roles of `ROLES.md` with one
kind of answer the survivor has: the ember skills, the statuses and schools,
the arts, the gear (affixes, Marks, sets and Named items from `docs/items`),
the blessings and the oaths. Where the probe could measure a cell, the cell
carries its number; where it could not, the verdict is reasoned from the code
and says so. §4 proposes the champion Signs, each with its counter.

## 1. How to read it

| Mark | Meaning | Measured as (probe, deft bot) |
|---|---|---|
| ●● | **hard counter**: the answer makes the role easy | ≥ 1.6× the median loadout |
| ● | **soft counter**: the answer is good against it | 1.2–1.6× |
| · | neutral | 0.8–1.2× |
| ○ | **weak against**: the role resists the answer | 0.4–0.8× |
| ○○ | **hard countered**: the role nearly stops the answer | < 0.4× |

The offence tables compare kills a minute (time to kill for elites and
champions) with the median of the fourteen single-skill loadouts against
the same creatures. The defence tables compare damage taken, inverted (2.0
means half the median hurt). All loadouts are one skill at rank 4 on a
warden's body at creature level 5 (`probe/README.md`).

**When is a lopsided cell good?** It is good when it is a **choice with
meaning**: the player can see it coming (the Wayfinder's table names the
people; the creature reads at a glance), the counter costs something
elsewhere, and the build without it is slower but not stuck. It is bad when
it is a **trap** (a build the draft handed the player dies to a creature it
cannot touch) or a **must-pick** (one answer so far ahead that nothing else
is a real choice). The rule from the research: hard counters are tolerable
where the player chose (`RESEARCH.md`, lessons 3, 7 and 20).

Bracketed cells are **stalemates, not safety**: a loadout that cannot kill
guards takes little damage because the slow guards never reach a survivor
who walks away, and nothing new arrives because nothing dies. In the arena,
where the horde keeps coming, that is the worst place to be.

### 2.3 What the matrix says

1. **The guard is the one hard counter, and it is aimed at a whole category.**
   Knifestorm kills 0.1× the median against guards, Rimeshard 0.4×, Volley
   0.5×; against a *champion* guard Volley takes 56 s, Knifestorm 91 s,
   Rimeshard 99 s. Verdict: **good for the rank and file, bad for
   champions.** A shieldman wall is a readable puzzle a projectile player can
   flank, freeze or out-wait, and the Risen and Kerchiefs always field softer
   kinds beside it. A champion behind an 80% guard is a wall. Fix: 60% for
   champions (`ROSTER.md`, tuning 2) and the "Shielded" Sign kept off
   creatures that already guard (§4).
2. **Lobbers hard-counter melee and the self-centred area.** Blades, Hallowed
   Ground and Axe Gyre kill **0.1–0.2×** the median of sappers and pillagers
   when the player stays off the burning ground. Verdict: **good**, because
   the counter is positional and the player can choose to stand in the fire
   for the kill (the plain bot, which does, kills them at the median rate and
   takes 2× the hurt). That is a real risk-for-reward trade at the scale of a
   single creature. Keep it; make sure every people with lobbers also brings
   melee-friendly kinds (all do).
3. **Ranged creatures soft-counter the self-centred area.** With a plain
   bot, Dawnpulse and Hallowed Ground kill **no** bowmen at all; the deft bot
   that walks out to them kills them at 0.9× and 1.4×. Verdict: **good**:
   it is the clearest skill check in the roster, and it is cheap to pass.
4. **Seeking Motes and Arcweb are generalists that never fall below
   neutral.** Motes is ●● or ● in eight of eleven rows and never worse than
   ○ (the champion guard); Arcweb is ●● in six. Verdict: **watch it**. Both
   ignore the guard (chains are not projectiles; motes home round shields),
   both seek, and Arcweb's boss damage is 3.5×. A generalist is healthy (the
   relaxed player needs one), but if they stay this far ahead the draft has
   a must-pick. The balance lab should look at them first.
5. **Axe Gyre kills everything near it and takes the most damage doing it.**
   It is ●● offence against fodder, swarmers, guards and elites and the
   worst defence in the table (0.3× against swarmers and guards). Verdict:
   **good**: the orbiting blades ask the player to stand in the crowd, and
   they pay for it. A gyre build is the purest sweaty loadout in the game.
6. **Rimeshard is the elite answer, on defence.** It kills elites no faster
   (0.8×) but halves the damage taken from them (2.3×) and from charging
   boars (1.9×): chill slows a creature by up to 60% and five chills freeze
   it (0.9 s for an elite), which also stops a lunge's wind-up and opens a
   guard. Verdict: **good**, and the best example of a counter that is not a
   damage number.
7. **Dawnpulse's knockback is the swarmer's answer, on defence.** Wolves hurt
   a Dawnpulse warden 1.7× less than the median, though it kills them slower.
   Verdict: **good**.
8. **Verdant Lance and Spirit Herd are weak almost everywhere at rank 4.**
   Lance is never above neutral on offence. Verdict: **bad** (a trap card),
   unless their evolutions change the picture; the balance lab should check
   them at rank 8.

**Lopsided and good:** frost against chargers and elites, holy against the
dead, damage-over-time against guards, fear against packs. Each is a
school's identity, each is visible on the Wayfinder's table ("their bane"),
and none leaves another school helpless.

**Watch:** the dead resist shadow by 35%, and four skills are shadow
(Umbral Bolt, Blightfield, Reaving Arc, Grave Tether), so a shadow build in a Risen arena is a slower one. That is fine as long as the
table says so (`DEPTH.md`: the resist readout).

## 4. Champion Signs (proposed)

Today a champion is its kind with three times the health. Signs give it one
extra verb, each with a tell and an answer. They follow the research's
lessons: one verb a Sign, telegraphed, no immunities, no reflect, at most
two on one creature, and every Sign answerable by at least two different
kinds of build (`RESEARCH.md`, lessons 1–9).

| Sign | What it does | Tell | Answered by | Soft for | Engine |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Swift** | +35% speed; its lunge or charge, if it has one, comes 30% more often | a streak of dust behind it | chill, knockback, a tree, Rimeshard, a vault | chargers already fast: never with the oath of the hunt | stat |
| **Ironbound** | takes 40% less from blows that are not critical; crits land in full | iron bands; sparks off it when a blow is turned | crits; Smoke Bomb's sure crits; Mark (the +30% still applies) | crit-less and damage-over-time builds (ticks never crit, so they are always cut) | `HitEnemy` (as `IronSkin`, per creature) |
| **Kindled** | leaves burning ground every 0.6 s (1.2 m, 3 s) | the ember's orange on its back | moving, fire resistance, ranged | – | `TrailSpec` (exists) |
| **Rimed** | its blows chill the survivor (slow 35% for 1.4 s) and its trail frosts | blue-white frost on it | tenacity, Sprint, Bull Rush, Surefooted | slow builds suffer most | `HitChill` per creature |
| **Bannered** | allies within 8 m are 25% faster and strike 15% more often; when it dies they flee for 2 s | a banner in its people's colour over it; the hastened glow | killing it first: Mark, Hunter's Mark, homing, Grapple | the relaxed player only meets a faster crowd | new `AuraSpec` |
| **Mending** | every 6 s, a 1 s cast heals allies within 7 m by 15% (never itself) | green ring on the cast | interrupts (Shield Bash, War Cry, Time Slip, Grapple, a freeze), burst | slow single-target builds | new `MendSpec` |
| **Gravebound** | raises two of the fallen near it every 8 s | the grave-caller's ring | interrupts; moving the fight off the corpses | – | `RaiseSpec` (exists) |
| **Brood** | dies into three lesser copies (fodder versions of its kind) | swollen, something moving under the skin | area; burning or hallowed ground where it dies | single-target builds take longer | `SplitSpec` (exists) |
| **Volatile** | dies into a 3 m blast after a 1 s fuse | a ticking glow that brightens as it weakens | a dash out; ranged kills; dragging it into the crowd (if the blast hurts the horde, see `IMPLEMENTATION.md` 9) | melee | `BurstSpec` (exists) |
| **Warded** | a ward that absorbs the next 6 blows (any size), back 8 s after it breaks | a ring of lamplight round it, one light per blow left | many small hits: knives, motes, gyres, damage over time | one-big-hit builds (Cinderfall, Bonesplitter) | new: a hit counter on `Enemy` |
| **Shielded** | gains a guard (1.5 rad, 60% off projectiles) | a shield on its arm | flanking, area, melee, freeze, stun | projectile builds; **never on a creature that already guards** | `GuardSpec` (exists) |
| **Leader** | its escort fights while it lives and **routs** (fear, 3 s) when it dies; it keeps 6 m back | a horn or a raised arm; the escort wears its colour | killing it first; it is softer than other champions (×2 health, not ×3) | – | `Fear` (exists) + `Behavior.Ranged` distance |

**Forbidden pairs** (never on one creature): Swift + Rimed (a fast slower is
inescapable), Swift + Volatile on a charger (a charge that ends in a blast),
Ironbound + Warded (two "your hits don't count" Signs), Shielded on a guard,
Mending + Leader (an unkillable back line), Brood + Gravebound (two
"more of them" Signs). **One aura Sign on screen at a time** (Bannered,
Mending), so the priority target is never ambiguous.

**How many.** Tier 1: champions bear no Sign until minute 10, then one.
Tier 2: one. Tier 3–4: one, two from minute 15. Tier 5+: two. Heralds: one
more than a champion at that tier, at most three. Signs never scale in
strength with tier, only in number (Diablo III's affix count by level, and
Path of Exile's lesson that stacking is what breaks them).

**Naming.** The champion's bar reads its Signs before its kind: "Swift,
Bannered Barrow Knight". The Sign's colour is on the bar and on the
creature, so the bar teaches the look.

## 6. Gear

What `docs/items` already plans, read against the roles. Gear is chosen by
day, knowing who holds the arena (the table names the people and their
bane), so a lopsided gear counter is the most acceptable kind of hard
counter in the game: it is scouting and preparing, StarCraft's kind
(`RESEARCH.md`, lesson 20).

### 6.4 Named and Storied items

Named items with a people's tag (Greymuzzle's Fang, Grimtunnel's Spare Lamp:
+30% less from lamplings) are slayers with a story: good. A Storied item is
a Named one lifted a grade by deeds; the deeds should include the bestiary:
**"killed a hundred champions of a people with it"** and **"won an arena of
that people at tier N with it"** make a counter-item grow from countering.
