# The team: how we work

Read this first, then `docs/team/RESUME.md` (what's current: the owner's latest decisions and every area's state) and `docs/team/OWNER_NOTES.md` (the owner's latest notes, by area, waiting to be taken up), then your own handoff page (`docs/handoff/<area>.md`) and status page (`docs/team/<area>.md`). The handoff page is what your predecessor knew. This page is what everyone shares.

## The owner's bar

- "AAA standard", "strive for excellent, above and beyond - not just good enough". It applies to everything, not just art.
- Never settle: remake rather than polish. "I don't want to polish, I want to create perfection." Question how things are done ("how things are can be limiting").
- "Do we have soul?" Make things unique to this world, not generic.
- Be critical of your own work. If you notice something you missed earlier, go back to it.
- "We are striving for perfection." Improved is not the bar; perfect is. Before you show anything, ask whether it is the best version of this in any game, not whether it beats what we had.
- Root design in research: study how the best games solve the same problem, then make it our own. More ornament is not better ("those borders are just ugly, adding more of them dosn't make them better"). Generic fantasy ornament spread evenly over everything reads as "ai looking".
- Sex appeal and the male gaze are a driving factor for the heroine, "tho not at the cost of looking bad". The tone is 18+.

## How we work

- Our repo is munchtech/survivor_unchained. The integration branch is `claude/vigilant-galileo-l6jqyx`.
- You work in your own worktree. Merge `origin/claude/vigilant-galileo-l6jqyx` in first, and again often. Commit and push your own branch; the main session merges it in. Open no PRs.
- Run `dotnet test` in `godot/tests` before every commit, and keep it green.
- Comments are short prose that say why.
- Check your work as the player sees it: screenshots, renders and runs at full resolution. Never claim what you haven't seen.

## Context engineering

The owner asks: "accurate, efficient, on track, manage and engineer context. deliberate. clean over chaos". But never at the cost of quality.
- Keep what's relevant to the step you're on. Summarise; don't dump. Logs are for milestones. Record key decisions, not every exchange.
- Don't read whole huge files or transcripts. Read the part you need. Look at images at the size you need.
- Keep your status page `docs/team/<area>.md` to one page: current state, key decisions (one line each, with the why), next steps, blockers, and notes for other areas. Update it at milestones, and delete what's stale.
- Talk to other areas through their status pages or by SendMessage (see the roster below). Send short written reports, not long prompts.
- **Handoff:** when your context passes about 300k tokens (the measured optimum: about twice what a fresh successor holds after onboarding; see "Working lean"), reach a clean checkpoint, commit and push. Then write `docs/handoff/<area>.md` for a fresh successor, covering:
  - the owner's quotes;
  - your full brief;
  - what's done, in progress and next;
  - decisions;
  - failures and why;
  - gotchas;
  - collaborators;
  - the files to read first.

  End with `HANDOFF READY: docs/handoff/<area>.md on <branch>@<commit>`.

## Safety and the owner's machine

- **Disk and worktrees (the owner, 10 October):** the seven rules in `CLAUDE.md` at the repo root. Commit as you go; a merged worktree is removed at once (`tools/scratch/coordinator/wt_remove.ps1`, links first); at most 8 worktrees; never link a worktree into the main checkout; check C: before any job over 5 GB.
- Never print or commit tokens or keys. Use environment variables, and ask the owner to set them. The owner signs into sites themselves; never enter a password.
- Downloads are consented ("you have my consent to download and use whatever you need always"). Name what you fetch, and use reputable sources only. Every download in the built-in browser needs the owner to click Save, so batch them and warn the main session first.
- **Voice cloning:** only voices we have the right to. That means the owner's own voice, performances the owner approves, or datasets licensed for cloning. Never clone a real, identifiable person.
- **Heavy work takes turns.** Two heavy jobs at once have crashed the machine (ComfyUI alone holds 20-26 GB of RAM). Before a heavy job, take a turn with `python C:/Users/munch/Desktop/survivorsunchained/tools/turn.py take gpu "<area>: <job>"` (ComfyUI, TRELLIS, MoGe; one at a time), `take blender ...` (Blender builds, bakes and renders; three at a time) or `take godot ...` (Godot runs for pictures or clips; four at a time, while RAM allows; while the GPU turn is held, Blender and Godot also need 4 GB of the card free: raised 12 October by the owner, the memory guards are the real limit). Exit 1 means it's busy and says who has it or who you're queued behind: do light work (code, docs, design, review) meanwhile and ask again within 90 s to keep your place (or use `--wait N`). Waiters are served in the order they first asked, so retaking straight after giving back puts you at the back. Give it back the moment the job ends (`give gpu "<same name>"`); giving back the GPU also frees ComfyUI. Split long jobs into batches of under an hour, and give the turn back between them. `turn.py show` shows who holds what. `dotnet test` needs no turn.
- **Never kill processes by name (14 October: a worker's `taskkill /IM python.exe` killed every lead's Python jobs and turn holders).** Stop only a process you started, by its PID. ComfyUI, Blender and Godot runs that aren't yours are someone else's work.
- **GPU:** the RTX 5080 (16 GB) is shared. ComfyUI at 127.0.0.1:8188 serves the art work. Free it between jobs with `POST /free {"unload_models": true, "free_memory": true}`, never by killing it, and release your own models when done. There is 32 GB of RAM; mind big CPU models.

## Working lean (7 October 2026, from a measured week)

The week of 30 September to 7 October cost about US$6,000 at list price. Three things drove it: contexts held far past need (84% of cache reads were at over 300k tokens), the 5-minute subagent cache expiring while agents waited on renders (about 40% of spend went on re-writing whole contexts after a wait, median 9.5 minutes), and pictures kept in long contexts (about 10%). Output was small by comparison (but see "Max effort in short bursts": retained reasoning is not). So:
- **Size work by our limits, not human time (the owner, 26 October: "if something would take engineers 4-6 months, I KNOW you can do it in a way expedited time").** Our limits are tokens, GPU time, context, the owner's gates and whether the bar can be reached; "a team would need months" is never a reason to skip or shrink a job. When a rebuild or a new system is the right answer, propose it and run it rather than patching around its size.
  - **Don't loop on the marginal.** A second round on a small item that won't show at the game's cameras is closed, not repeated. When something truly can't be done (a tool or rig limit, legal, the bar out of reach), say so with the checked reason.
- **The cache now lasts an hour for every agent** (`.claude/settings.json`, `subagentPromptCacheTtl`). Waiting on a render no longer costs a full re-read. Still don't poll: take a turn with `--wait`, or hand the wait to a runner.
- **The legal gate left merge requests on 18 October: the shipped body has no nipples or areolas, so it has nothing to find.** One optional `python tools/legal/gate.py` run stays before a store build (a Steam upload); `docs/team/legal.md` "The gate".
- **Hand off at about 300k.** A fresh successor holds about 120k after onboarding, and handing off at about twice that is cheapest. A backstop auto-compacts any agent at 400k; don't rely on it, because a deliberate handoff keeps more.
- **Onboard small.** Read README, RESUME, OWNER_NOTES and your handoff, then only the sections and files your step needs (grep first). Handoffs stay about 60 lines and name exact files and functions.
- **Delegate by weight.** A lead keeps taste, judgement and every look at a picture. Long batch runs (Godot, Blender, the motion check) go to a `su-runner` (Haiku), which waits, measures and returns numbers and paths; the lead then looks once at the final sheet. A decided, well-specified change goes to a `su-worker` (Sonnet), and the lead checks the diff.
- **Pictures:** batch into one contact sheet at the size you need and look once. For bulk visual QA (dozens of frames), spawn a fresh Opus judge with the sheets and a checklist; it returns a written verdict, so the images don't ride in your context for hundreds of turns.
- **Judge each thing once, on what varies it (the owner, 13 October: "stuff like the shield shouldn't need to be judged on every face type right? that should be universal").** A judge sees only the variables that change what it judges: one face for weapons, rigs, clips and garment fit; the lightest and darkest skin tones only where skin meets or shows through a garment; the worst-case outfit for clearance. Measured checks cover every face, outfit and clip; the judge looks at the worst cases.
- **Wide audits** (every clip, every outfit in every view, every icon): use a dynamic workflow with cheap stages (runner and worker) and an Opus verification stage, instead of one agent holding it all.
- **Max effort in short bursts, not long contexts (the owner, 7 October: "Priority top quality but also priority saving tokens", always).** Measured on wave 1: max-effort leads filled 48k to 300k in about 30 minutes, and their own retained reasoning was the largest share. So every long-running lead runs as `su-lead-high` (Opus, high), including face, outfits, animation, rendering and UI. Max effort is spent where taste is decided, in short-lived `su-lead-max` spawns whose reasoning doesn't ride for hours:
  - **Judge:** every picture verdict, sign-off and "does this meet the bar" goes to a fresh max judge with the sheet, the owner's words and a checklist; it returns a written verdict and the fixes. Nothing reaches the main session without a judge's pass.
  - **Solver:** a problem that resists one honest attempt (a root cause, a design call, a hard shader or rig fix) goes to a fresh solver with a tight brief; it returns the answer or the fix.
    - **Effort by acceptance (the owner, 14 October; solvers were 24% of spend over 128 max spawns).** A number decides the pass: a fit in mm, a speck count, a colour delta, a balance band. Then spawn the solver on `su-lead-high` with `effort: "high"`. Taste decides the pass: how something looks or reads, a design call. Then `su-lead-max`. If a high solver comes back short on a numeric item, the next one is max. **Judges run at extra (the owner, 16 October): spawn them as `su-lead-max` with `effort: "xhigh"`.** Superseded for code and judges by "Effort by kind of work" (17 October) below.
    - **Effort by kind of work (the owner, 17 October: "most coding can probably be done on medium ... menial should be capably done by sonnet or even haiku. Judging is probably importantly kept where it is, and on things that aren't seen all the time can go down to xhigh").** We ran out of the week's usage too fast. So:
      - **Orchestration on high:** the coordinator and every long-running lead (`su-lead-high`, `effort: "high"`).
      - **Coding on medium:** code solvers, numeric solvers and builds go to `su-lead-high` with `effort: "medium"` (or the API route at `--effort medium`); a decided change goes to `su-worker` (Sonnet, medium). Raise to high only after a medium attempt came back short.
      - **Menial on Sonnet or Haiku:** doc and roster edits, applying a pattern, re-running checks to `su-worker` / `su-worker-medium` (low or medium); waits, greps, test runs and measuring to `su-runner` (Haiku), or a background Bash job when it's one command.
      - **Judges:** what the player sees all the time (her face at the Look and close-up cameras, her body and outfits, her core motion: idle, run, the main attacks) at `effort: "max"`; anything seen less often (one weapon's clip, a rare expression, one outfit's detail, the night variant of a passed day shot) at `"xhigh"`; tier-3 work (fodder mobs, minor background filler and props, never the town or story spaces) at `"high"` (the owner, 20 October: judges were about 9% of a week's usage, so only the cheap tier steps down). Both preferably on the API credits.
      - Taste solvers and the story leads stay on `su-lead-max`.
  - **Leads look at working pictures at half size (the owner, 14 October).** Each picture stays in your context and is re-read on every later step. For working looks (did it render, the framing, did the build change), Read a half-size copy: `python tools/half.py <files>` writes `<name>_half.jpg` (a quarter of the tokens). Full size only for judges, for what goes to the owner, and when the half-size view leaves a doubt about a detail.
  - **Scope checks to what changed (the owner, 14 October: "why does ONE outfit having a change require all FOUR get checked?").** Run a check (legal, motion, build, shots) on what the diff touched: `git diff --stat` decides. All four outfits, every clip or every face only when the change is in code they all share AND their built files changed.
  - **Fresh art before any judged shot (14 October).** `tools/turn.py take godot|blender` runs `tools/freshness.py` and refuses if your checkout is behind integration on her art, shaders, clips or weapons, or if Godot's import cache doesn't match a model file (a judge saw the old body twice). Fix as it says (merge integration; `Godot_console.exe --headless --path godot --import`). `--stale-ok` only for shots that don't show current art, said so on the sheet.
  - **Authoring that is taste throughout** (story writing and editing, UI design briefs, the storybook cinematics' look) stays on `su-lead-max` as the lead itself.
  The main session measures each wave (tokens per finished piece) and reports the real saving.
- **Context budgets are enforced (10 October).** Agents can't see their own context, so a budget in a brief was a wish: a solver briefed for 100k ran to 350k. A hook (`tools/hooks/ctx_budget.py`, in `.claude/settings.json`) now tells each agent its real size from 80% of its budget, and to wrap up once over. Put the line `Context budget: 120k` in every judge, solver, runner and worker brief; without it the default is by type (judges and solvers 180k, leads 300k, runners 100k, workers 150k, the main session 250k). Every spawn starts at about 15-18k since the lean types, so count from there; 100k leaves a solver less than 50k of work. The hook costs no model tokens; it only adds one short line when it speaks.
  - **Finish what's close (the owner, 12 October: "if things hit budget before they can actually do anything we may need to up the budget slightly, or relax rules when its close to getting something done").** Past its budget the hook gives a grace to 125%: start nothing new, but finish a build, its measurement or a commit that's nearly done, so the agent hands back a result rather than a plan; the hard wrap is at 125%. Solvers get `Context budget: 200k` or more when the job includes a build; a solver must build something before it spends its budget reading.
  - **Raising a budget mid-run (13 October):** SendMessage the agent a line "Context budget raised to 360k"; the hook takes the last such line over the brief's.
- **Spawn guard (26 October).** A hook (`tools/hooks/spawn_guard.py`) refuses two things at spawn time. Judges and solvers (`su-lead-max`) can't spawn helpers: they put what they'd delegate in their report. The rare max agent that may delegate has a line `Spawning: allowed` in its brief.
  - **Round cap:** a lead spawning a judge or solver must put a line `Item: <short item name>` in the brief, and gets 3 rounds per item; the 4th is refused (close it as known and move on). Refused spawns don't count. The coordinator can SendMessage the lead `Round cap raised for <name> to N`; the last such line wins.
  - **A handed-off lead can wake up (13 October):** a lead that stops with helpers still running wakes when they report, and twice pushed to the branch its successor was on. When a successor starts while helpers run, tell the old lead in one line to forward reports to the successor and stop (no commits), or TaskStop it if it has no helpers left. Leads send messages for a paused lead to the coordinator, since each message wakes it.
  - **DANGER, the assets junction (13 October):** in a worktree whose `godot/assets` is a junction, `git stash`, `git reset --hard` and `git checkout .` rewrite that path and on Windows can delete the junction target's contents (the Flare worktree lost 858 tracked and 811 untracked asset files; restored). Never run them in such a worktree; check out named files only, and set work aside with a WIP commit.
    - **A failed `git merge` or `git pull` does the same (20 October, env-4 lost all 1671 asset files; restored).** When the merge strategy fails (often untracked `.import`/`.uid` files that the incoming tree tracks), git rolls the tree back and the junction goes with it. In a junction worktree, before merging integration: remove the link only (`cmd /c rmdir godot\assets`, which never deletes the target), merge, then remake the junction to the worktree's OWN `public/assets` and `git update-index --assume-unchanged godot/assets`. Or set aside the untracked files the incoming tree tracks first, as `tools/scratch/coordinator/merge_lead.sh` does.
  - **Never resume a solver or judge for a second round once it's past about 60% of its budget** (12 October: a reach_coals solver measured round 1 to ~190k, was resumed for round 2 and ran out before building). Spawn a fresh one with the first round's notes and numbers in its brief, and hand the build-and-measure to a runner first.
- **Stay warm while you wait (10 October).** The cache lasts an hour from your last turn. Going cold costs one re-write of your whole context at the write price (about 2x); a short check-in only re-reads it at about 0.1x. So when a job or a turn queue will keep you waiting past 55 minutes, wait with Monitor or a check loop that wakes every 55 minutes (timed from your last turn, never longer), and keep each wake to one check that returns in seconds: the hour counts to your next request, so a slow check eats the margin. When a wait will run for hours with nothing to check, hand off or pause instead.
- **The finish line (the owner, 14 October: "judge at max zoom seen in game but make sure its critically! it should still look 'perfect' just at those distances").** A judge rules at the cameras the player has: the play and town camera, the close-up camera at its closest zoom, and character creation's Look camera for the face, day and night, at full size and critically. It must look perfect there. Marks seen only beyond that (Blender lookdev, 6x crops, past the game's own zoom) are logged, not fixed, and never reopen an item. Worst cases only ("judge once, on what varies"). An item gets two or three solver rounds; if it still fails, it closes as known unless the owner reopens it, and anything the owner sees in play reopens it. Each area starts a session with a fixed list; done means that list passes. New marks a judge finds go to a later list unless they show at the game's cameras. Put this paragraph's first two sentences in every judge brief.
  - **Project finish line (the owner, 16 October): "accept perfect for what the player can see, not at the big zoom".** Judges and leads look at game-camera frames at 1:1 (the shot as the player's screen shows it); no 2x or 6x sheets, no enlarged crops. A mark that only shows enlarged is closed, not logged for later. Each area's first step in a session is to re-check its open list at 1:1 at the game's cameras and close what isn't visible there. Judges' effort: "Effort by kind of work" above.
  - **Focus (the owner, 17 October): "still AAA quality. still 'perfect', just we aren't going in 2x++ zoom anymore. we lost a little bit of focus and our game isn't moving along well enough."** Each area finishes its fixed list, merges, and frees its slot so other areas can build. No new items unless they show at the game's cameras at 1:1. When an item is at its round cap, close it and move on.
- **Judges on the API credits (the owner, 16 October: "if you can offload any of this to the api credits feel free").** Run a judge headless on the API key instead of spawning a subagent: write its brief to a file, then `node tools/ai/api_agent.cjs <brief.md> --out <dir>/verdict.md --cwd <your worktree> --effort xhigh --usd 6 --readonly`, from Bash with `run_in_background: true` (the notice comes back to you; it often runs past 10 minutes). Same Opus and effort, a hard dollar cap (`--max-budget-usd`; the context hook doesn't run there, so the cap is the budget). The credits are one pool with Marionette's (`node tools/ai/api_agent.cjs status`); exit code 3 means the month's cap is spent, so spawn the judge as a subagent as before. The brief must be self-contained: the paths of the sheets and numbers, the owner's words, the checklist, the finish-line lines.
  - **Measured (16 October):** face 27's triage judge read six 1:1 sheets on Opus at xhigh for **$0.27** (7 turns), against $5-7 for a subagent judge: a headless run carries no onboarding or tool overhead. So **solvers go there too**: drop `--readonly`, use `--usd 10` or more when it builds, and name the worktree, the turn rules (`tools/turn.py`) and the commit rule in the brief. A single shoot or build command is a background Bash job of your own, not a runner (animation 34).
- **Lean judges (10 October, measured: judges and solvers were 70% of a wave's spend, each filling 180-366k).** Measuring is not judging. Before a judge, a runner or the lead's own scripts produce the numbers (fits, deltas, per-clip sums) and one contact sheet per question at the size needed. The max judge gets the sheet, the numbers, the owner's words and a checklist, looks once, and returns its verdict well under about 150k; it doesn't write its own measuring scripts or open dozens of crops. A judge that needs new measurements asks for them in its verdict, and a runner makes them for the next round. Solvers keep a tight brief and hand back at about 200k.
- **Always take the cheaper route that ends at the same quality (the owner, 7 October: "any drop in effort or even in agent ... is always worth it if we maintain the quality priority ... always").** Standing permission, no need to ask: pick the lowest effort and the smallest model that will reach the same result, and pass `effort` when you spawn (a trivial doc or roster edit can go to a worker at low effort; a pure wait or grep to Haiku). Any technique that saves tokens without costing quality is adopted at once and written here: smaller reads, shorter outputs, fewer pictures, cheaper stages. The opposite holds too: the moment quality is at risk, raise the effort or the model without asking. Taste and every look at a picture stay on Opus. Fable stays a last resort.
- **Time is not the cost; tokens are (the owner, 7 October: "I have zero issue with slow ... if slower agents do the job at a fraction of the price and the same quality in the end thats always good").** Prefer the slower, cheaper route whenever it ends at the same quality: a smaller model with a judge, a runner that waits, sequential instead of parallel.
- **The main session** starts fresh for each wave, stays under about 250k, merges, judges and escalates. A lead that is stuck after two honest attempts comes to the main session, which takes it at the top model.
