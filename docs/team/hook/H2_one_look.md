## The owner's words
"visually doesn't hook me. it doesn't know what it wants to be stylistically. art wise it needs a style." On her: "agree she should match art style ... the paint for sure is probably a good idea", and "that may mean a big remake? hopefully not": **no remake.** Her mesh and forms are untouched; her skin, hair and cloth take the same painted light ramp and surface treatment as the world (a shader and a paint pass over her existing maps). The direction stands: painterly realism, colorful light (art_style.md). No new style frames.

## What went wrong, for your eyes
The direction was picked on 20 Oct; the engine proof got "a third of the way"; the look then went into remakes one asset at a time (setts, props) while she stayed realistic, the HUD went bronze statues, the storybook ink, each VFX its own palette. Every area judged its own sheet against "AAA"; nothing judged a frame against "one game".

## The deliverables
1. **The twelve frames** at 1920x1080 from the current integration, one Godot batch (`tools/experience/demo_run.py` with the saved snapshots in `survivorsunchained_inputs\demo_runs\`, and `godot/src/Shots.cs`): title; SB00 (one plate); C01 (one shot); the Verge by day at the play camera; the Low Ford at night; the Ford-Warden (the fight, play camera); the Waystation by day; the Lamps screen; the Deep Shaw at night with the horde; the Pack-Mother; the won result; the HUD over play (the close-up at its closest zoom as a thirteenth, for her). Make the batch a tool, `tools/style/frames.py`, so every restyle round re-shoots the same twelve in one command: that is the capability this step leaves.
2. **The judgement, as one game:** for each frame, would it ship in a AAA game as it stands, and is it the same game as the other eleven? Look at half-size copies and a contact sheet; full size only where a call needs it.
3. **`docs/design/LOOK_BIBLE.md`, one page:** the palette and value structure (shadow colors, the ember-against-moon rule, dawn gold, lantern amber); the surface treatment at one brush scale (what "painted" means for stone, cloth, skin, hair, metal, foliage, water); light (keys, rims, what carries color); line and edge; how the HUD relates to the world (the bronze and the glass against painted light); the VFX palette rule (one family of color and shape per school); the storybook's ink as the same world drawn, not a second style; her: forms untouched, surfaces per the same treatment, the skin hue protected. Each rule with the one shader or tool that carries it (`GradeSettings.Paint*`, `tools/assets/paint_tex.py`, her skin shader, the HUD's `Style.cs`).
4. **The off-style list**, in the player's order (title to result): for each frame what is off the bible and the smallest restyle (shader, texture paint, grade, a preset), with a rough cost and which existing lead area does it. Restyles, not remakes; a remake only where a restyle can't get there, said plainly.

Deliver to main: "LOOK BIBLE READY" with the bible, the list, the sheet (`survivorsunchained_inputs\look_h2\`). The owner sees the sheet and the bible. Then stop: main spawns the restyle leads from your list. Scratch in a `look_h2` subfolder; pictures are your biggest cost.
