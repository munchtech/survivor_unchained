# F3 — ARPG horde-arena modes and famous missing bosses

Research strand for the Survivor Unchained boss-fight design document. Every factual claim carries a URL. "(secondary)" marks a claim seen only in a search-engine summary, not on the fetched page itself. "Not found" means I looked and could not confirm a number.

Note on sources: poewiki.net (the current community PoE wiki) sits behind an Anubis/Cloudflare bot wall and refused both HTML and API fetches, so Path of Exile 1 facts come from the older Fandom wiki via its MediaWiki API (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/api.php), official GGG forum posts and guide sites.

## Part 1 — ARPG horde-arena modes

### 1.1 Path of Exile: the Simulacrum (Delirium)

**What it is.** A map fragment (300 Simulacrum Splinters) that opens 6 portals into a wave arena built on a town layout: Lunacy's Watch, The Bridge Enraptured, The Syndrome Encampment, Hysteriagate or Oriath Delusion (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Simulacrum). It was introduced in 3.10.0 (Delirium league, 2020) (same source).

**Wave count — correcting the brief.** The brief says "20 Delirium waves". That was true at launch: GGG's April 2020 statistics post says 57% of players who started a Simulacrum completed "all 20 waves" (https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2828348). Patch 3.16.0 then added 10 extremely hard extra waves, making 30, with "escalating chances for a Delirium boss to spawn" and Delirium uniques past wave 20 (secondary; search summary of https://poedb.tw/us/Simulacrum). The Fandom wiki now gives 30 waves (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Simulacrum).

**Pacing and pause.** The player starts each wave manually by interacting with the "Strangely Familiar Entity"; each wave raises difficulty and rewards; higher waves add monster modifiers and raise the Delirium level. Loot drops when the wave is fully cleared, and there is a stash in the zone, but "starting a new wave will destroy all items on the ground" (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Simulacrum). So the downtime between waves is player-controlled — the opposite of a survivors-style clock — and is used for looting and re-gearing.

**How bosses arrive.** Bosses are not on a fixed beat but on a *probability window with a guaranteed backstop*:
- Omniphobia, Fear Manifest "can start spawning" at wave 10 and is guaranteed at wave 15 if it has not already spawned (https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2828348).
- Kosis, The Revelation can spawn from wave 15 and is guaranteed at wave 20 if it has not (same source).
- Delirium bosses have an increased chance to spawn from wave 18 onward (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Simulacrum), and can spawn several times per Simulacrum (the wiki marks this "citation needed", same source).

**Where the difficulty bites.** GGG's own data on 813,604 Simulacrum instances: waves 1–9 were completed at 99.95–99.98%, wave 10 at 99.86%, wave 15 at 96.35% and wave 20 at 76.25%; the sharpest spikes are at waves 15 and 18, with wave 20 combining "maximum Deliriousness" with the guaranteed boss (https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/2828348). In other words, the drop-off coincides with the boss guarantees — boss arrival *is* the difficulty curve.

**Design takeaways.** (a) "Can spawn from X, guaranteed by Y" gives tension without letting the boss be fully predictable. (b) Publishing completion-by-wave telemetry showed the designers exactly where players broke; the same telemetry would be worth logging per minute in an ember arena.

### 1.2 Path of Exile: Ultimatum and the Trialmaster

**Structure.** The Trialmaster and an altar spawn in a zone; the altar shows a reward, an objective and three modifiers, and the player must pick one modifier before starting (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Ultimatum). Four objectives: defeat waves; survive until the timer runs out; stand in stone circles; protect the altar (same source).

**The gamble.** After each wave the player chooses to cash out or to start another wave by *adding* a new modifier. Dying, leaving the circle too long, or reaching 7 stacks of Ruin fails the trial and removes all rewards (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Ultimatum). A guide sums up the stakes: "Failure means you walk away from the Ultimatum encounter with nothing to show for your time" (https://poe-vault.com/guides/ultimatum-league-mechanic-guide). A trial lasts 3 to 9 waves, with more waves at higher zone levels (Fandom wiki above).

**How modifiers stack.** Modifiers are permanent for the run and accumulate; many come in tiers I–III, and picking the same family again escalates it (for example Choking Miasma: a chaos cloud follows you → a large cloud → a large and fast cloud; Stalking Ruin: an invulnerable shade stalks you and inflicts Ruin → gains speed and casts spells → gains further speed with powerful spells) (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Ultimatum). Some modifiers are clocks in their own right: "Escalating Damage Taken" raises damage taken by 1% per second up to 50%; "Escalating Monster Speed" gives monsters 2.5% more attack/cast/move speed per second alive, up to 50% (same source). Others attack the build directly: "Occasional Impotence" (you and your minions deal no damage for 2 seconds every 8 seconds), "Less Cooldown Recovery" (cooldowns take five times longer), "Random Projectiles" (your projectiles fly in random directions) (same source). In parties, each player gets their own rewards and players vote on the next modifier; ties are broken randomly (same source).

**How the boss arrives.** In red-tier maps (area level 78+) there is a chance of a 10th wave that teleports the player to the Trialmaster's arena (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Ultimatum, citing https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3129971). The wiki gives 1% at tiers 11–13 and 2% at tier 14+ but flags this "citation needed" (same source). The key design point: **every modifier you accepted still applies in the boss fight** (https://poe-vault.com/guides/ultimatum-league-mechanic-guide), and hazard-type modifiers that normally ring the altar move into the centre of the small circular arena (secondary; search summary of poewiki.net/wiki/Ixchel). Ruin-inflicting modifiers "can easily cause you to fail a protracted Boss fight" (poe-vault, above). Abilities reported include Abyss Slam, a moving Cyclone and a charged red Shockwave (secondary; same search summary). Patch 3.14.1 "added a short grace period when being teleported to The Trialmaster's arena" (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Trialmaster) — a small but telling fix: a teleport-into-boss needs a beat of safety.

**Narrative frame.** Narrative designer Matt Dymerski explained that the Trialmaster remembers *the player's* record across characters, not the character's: "He's not talking to your character. He's talking to you!", and Chaos is the one entity in Wraeclast aware of the timelines (https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3106179). His barks change with your win/loss record — "How do you keep winning?!" (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Trialmaster). Forum commenters on that post complained the article omitted fight mechanics (same forum thread, as summarised on fetch).

**Design takeaways.** (a) The boss fight is the *sum of your choices*: the curses you drafted follow you in. That is a strong match for an ember arena where curses or "embers of ruin" could be drafted alongside weapons. (b) A per-run fail counter (7 Ruin) separate from HP gives a second, readable loss condition. (c) Grace period on arrival.

### 1.3 Path of Exile: Blight

**Structure.** A tower-defence encounter: interacting with a Fungal Growth makes Sister Cassia place a Purification Pump, which spawns roots in random directions; at the end of each root a portal spawns monsters that walk slowly to the core, "only stopping to attack units in its way" (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Blight_(game_mechanic)). Up to 8 roots, more at higher zone level; some roots open after a delay; some carry a density multiplier; an icon over a root shows which tower type it resists (same source). There is a short preparation period which the player may skip (same source).

**Damage and fail state.** The pump has 10 durability; a monster that reaches it costs 1 (normal), 2 (magic), 5 (rare) or 10 (unique), so a single unique reaching the pump ends the encounter (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Blight_(game_mechanic)). Rare and unique enemies are faster, tougher and may run past minions (the wiki marks the last point unconfirmed) (same source). Each lane cleared spawns a chest at its portal, and those chests persist even if the pump later dies (same source) — partial success is banked.

**Blighted Maps (the big version).** A dedicated map with natives removed and one Fungal Growth spawning dozens of roots; the player defends the pump for 5 minutes, then must kill everything left; preparation time is longer and pump durability is 20 instead of 10 (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Blighted_Map). Anointing the map with oils trades difficulty for reward (same source).

**Bosses.** Blight's "bosses" are unique-rarity blight monsters walking the lanes (the minimap has a dedicated BlightBoss icon) (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Blight_league); a unique reaching the pump deals the full 10 (https://pathofexile.fandom.com/wiki/Blight_(game_mechanic)). Exact unique blight boss rosters and spawn timings: not found on the fetched pages.

**Design takeaways.** (a) A boss that does not fight you but *walks past you* toward an objective changes the player's question from "can I survive" to "can I stop it" — a useful variant for one ember-arena boss (e.g. a herald marching on the brazier). (b) Lane-resistance icons are a pre-warning that tells the player what to build.

### 1.4 Path of Exile 2: Trial of Chaos

**Structure.** Rounds depend on area level: 4 rounds (area level 39–59), 7 (60–74), 10 (75+); after round 10 the player may wager currency to extend to up to 30 rounds (https://maxroll.gg/poe2/resources/trial-of-chaos). Objectives rotate: defeat all monsters; survive until the timer expires; escort the statue or collect the Soul Cores; defeat the boss — with boss rounds at 4, 7 and 10 (same source).

**Modifiers.** Before every round the player picks 1 of 3 negative modifiers "that are going to affect the rest of the run"; they stack for the whole trial; there are more than 30 (https://maxroll.gg/poe2/resources/trial-of-chaos). Failing any round loses all gathered loot (same source).

**Bosses as keys.** Round-10 bosses are Uxmal (the Beastlord), Bahlak (the Sky Seer) and Chetza (the Feathered Plague); each drops a "Fate", and three Fates (Cowardly, Deadly, Victorious) unlock the Trialmaster himself, a fight that "emphasizes dodge mechanics against expanding damage zones" (https://maxroll.gg/poe2/resources/trial-of-chaos). Patch 0.5.5 reportedly reworked the mode to one chest per room, allowed leaving at any time and gave 2 retries per Inscribed Ultimatum (secondary; title of https://www.playnews.gg/en/guides/path-of-exile-2-trial-of-chaos-revamped-in-0-5-5-one-chest-per-room-exit-possible-at-any-time-and-2-retries-per-inscribed-ultimatum) — i.e. GGG softened the all-or-nothing loss.

**Design takeaways.** (a) Boss rounds on a fixed rhythm (4/7/10) inside a wave mode, very close to "blessing at 0:00, blessing at 15:00, boss at 30:00". (b) Losing everything on one mistake was walked back with retries and banked chests — a warning against all-or-nothing ember runs.

### 1.5 Path of Exile 2: Trial of the Sekhemas

**Structure.** 4 floors; each is a run of 7 rooms ending in a boss and reward chests (https://maxroll.gg/poe2/resources/trial-of-the-sekhemas). Room types: Escape, Ritual, Chalice, Hourglass (a timed wave-survival room) and Gauntlet (same source).

**Honour.** A second health bar: "The moment your Honour reaches 0, your run is over" — Armour reduces Honour loss and Evasion avoids it (https://maxroll.gg/poe2/resources/trial-of-the-sekhemas). Boons (buffs, some bought from merchants) and Afflictions (debuffs) accrue from rooms; Relics give passive bonuses; Bronze/Silver/Gold keys open chests (same source).

**Bosses.** Floor 1 Rattlecage, the Earthbreaker; floor 2 the duo Hadi of the Flaming River and Rafiq of the Frozen Spring; floor 3 Ashar, the Sand Mother; floor 4 Zarokh, the Temporal (pinnacle) (https://maxroll.gg/poe2/resources/trial-of-the-sekhemas).

**Player opinion.** See section 1.8.

**Design takeaways.** Honour separates "being hit" from "being killed": an ember arena could make the ember itself a second bar that the boss attacks.

### 1.6 Diablo IV: Infernal Hordes and the Fell Council

**Structure.** Introduced with Season 5 (patch 1.5.0, August 2024). Players open an Infernal Compass and fight time-limited waves; Maxroll's current guide gives waves of 60 seconds, compasses rolling 6, 8 or 10 waves, and pre-Torment "Fleeting" compasses with 4 waves (https://maxroll.gg/d4/resources/infernal-hordes). The 1.5.0 patch notes record PTR-driven changes: "Tiers 1-3: 6 Waves, Tiers 4-5: 7 Waves", the wave timer reduced to 60 seconds, more monsters, Council and boss health pools reduced, and "distinct audio queues [sic] have been added to signal the beginning and end of a wave" (https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24123440/diablo-iv-1-5-0-patch-notes). The PTR wave length was 90 seconds (secondary; search summary of https://www.charlieintel.com/diablo/diablo-4-season-5-release-date-everything-we-know-330319/ and others).

**Burning Aether.** A run-scoped currency dropped by Aether Fiends, Aether Lords, Soulspires and Aetheric Masses; it "only lasts as long as your run does, and if you don't spend it, you lose it when the event ends" (https://maxroll.gg/d4/resources/infernal-hordes). Aether Goblins drop 25 each (same source). After the boss, Aether is spent on the "Spoils of Hell" chests (materials, gold, guaranteed Ancestral Legendaries), and beating the Council adds a large Aether drop that scales with difficulty (same source; also https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24123440/diablo-iv-1-5-0-patch-notes).

**Infernal Offers — how modifiers stack.** Between waves the player picks one of three offers, each a boon/bane pair, and "these Infernal Offers stack cumulatively across your run. The Offer you take at the end of Wave 1 still applies in Waves 2 and onwards" (https://maxroll.gg/d4/resources/infernal-hordes). Examples: "The Exalted Fiends" (Aether Fiends +100% health | Fiends grant +2 Aether); "The Ruthless Lords" (Lords gain health/damage per spawn | +5 Aether) (same source). Unlike Ultimatum, the bane is paired with a *reward-density* boon, so the gamble is "harder for more currency" rather than "harder for one more chest". Chaos Waves sometimes replace the three offers with a special wave (Aether Goblins, Defend the Mass, Carry the Reliquary, Hell Wyrms, the Burning Butcher) (same source).

**How the boss arrives.** After the last wave the player walks to the Well of Hatred and fights the Fell Council (secondary; search summary of Season 5 coverage, e.g. https://shacknews.com/article/140867/diablo-4-1-5-0-patch-notes). The Council is three of five members chosen at random: Geleb the Flame (three-headed hydras, meteors, flame waves), Wyand the Shadow (shadow hydras, teleport), Ismail the Accursed (slow-projectile hydras and destructible "Claws"), Maffer the Cruel (charges, Bone Prison) and Bremm the Maelstrom (lightning hydras, chain lightning, summons Fallen Lunatics) (https://maxroll.gg/d4/resources/infernal-hordes). A later addition lets the player spend 666 Burning Aether to fight Bartuc instead, a fire-and-shadow caster "with a chaotic intermission", for better rewards (same source) — the boss as a purchasable upgrade paid for with the run's own currency.

**Timing.** Maxroll's first-week write-up notes the Council arrives "after 15 minutes of gameplay", and that dying there wastes the entire run: "If you die more than once every 3 runs, you should probably farm a lower tier" (https://maxroll.gg/d4/news/infernal-hordes-first-week-impressions). That is the same shape as the ember arena — a long horde build-up with all stakes concentrated on one boss at the end.

**Player opinion.** Maxroll: "Yes. In fact, Infernal Hordes are too good" — items about twice Helltide, gold about 20x Whispers, materials about 3x the Pit; on offers, "you just go for Hellborne every time" (https://maxroll.gg/d4/news/infernal-hordes-first-week-impressions). Mein-MMO reports the mode was disliked on the PTR and then called "the best thing that could happen" after Blizzard's changes, with Reddit praising the density (secondary; https://mein-mmo.de/diablo-4-infernal-hordes-erst-unbeliebt-jetzt-das-beste-was-passieren-konnte/). The "always pick Hellborne" consensus is a warning: if one offer is dominant, the draft between waves stops being a decision.

### 1.7 Grim Dawn: The Crucible (brief)

Originally a Kickstarter stretch goal called "Survival Mode" (https://grimdawn.fandom.com/wiki/The_Crucible). Three difficulties (Aspirant, Challenger, Gladiator), each 150 waves (170 with Ashes of Malmouth), across ten selectable arenas (same source). Every 10 waves the player may cash out or continue; death ends the run (same source). Tributes, earned every 10 waves, buy restarts at checkpoints (wave 50 or 100), arcane Defences (beacons and banners, each upgradable twice) and Celestial Blessings costing 12 Tributes that last 25 minutes but expire on death (same source). Mutators start at wave 31 and re-randomise every 10 waves, rising from 1 to a maximum of 5 (at waves 31/61/91/111/131); each is two-sided, e.g. "Swift" (+8/12/15% movement speed but −15% crowd-control resistance) or "Leeching" (heal from attacks but −10% health) (same source). Bosses: "the farther you go… the possibility of encountering multiple bosses at once" (secondary; search summary of Crucible coverage, e.g. https://www.gamebanshee.com/kwkq3); a specific boss-wave schedule was not found.

**Design takeaways.** Time-limited blessings bought with arena currency are a close cousin of the ember arena's great blessings. Two-sided mutators ("stronger in one way, weaker in another") let a counter-build emerge, rather than only stacking pain.

### 1.8 Player opinion across PoE modes

- Trial of the Sekhemas was widely criticised as punishing, especially for melee; GGG responded by making Honour damage scale with distance ("in close range you'll take 35% less Honour Damage") and fixing a bug where damage over time dealt three times the intended Honour damage (secondary; search summary of https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/path-of-exile-2-patch-fixes-endgame-pain-points-3009584 and https://pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3642235). Some players still found it unforgiving (secondary; https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3642482/page/6).
- PoE2 Trial of Chaos was later loosened (exit at any time, one chest per room, retries) (secondary; https://www.playnews.gg/en/guides/path-of-exile-2-trial-of-chaos-revamped-in-0-5-5-one-chest-per-room-exit-possible-at-any-time-and-2-retries-per-inscribed-ultimatum).
- The repeated lesson: all-or-nothing loss on a single death at the end of a long horde run is what players complain about most, and developers have repeatedly softened it.

## Part 2 — Famous bosses the earlier notes missed

### 2.1 Enter the Gungeon

**The Resourceful Rat.** The Rat is a recurring NPC before he is a boss: he steals any item left on the floor (but never health, blanks, keys, credits or Glass Guon Stones) and leaves a note thanking the player before insulting them (https://enterthegungeon.fandom.com/wiki/Resourceful_Rat). The boss was added in the Advanced Gungeons & Draguns update and waits at the end of a maze below the Black Powder Mine (same source). It is a three-phase fight that changes *genre* each phase: phase 1 is a bullet-hell duel with mousetraps, cheese spirals, kunai and a bullet whip (1,480 HP); after it he flees down a hole the player must jump into; phase 2 is a stationary mech built from stolen loot (3,515 HP) whose two shoulder weapon systems can be destroyed, swapping its attacks for easier ones; phase 3 is a *Punch-Out!!* parody with three health bars, interruptible "bold" attacks that charge a super punch, and a timer (same source). In phase 3, losing does not kill you — "the Rat will simply run away and leave a note" — and every successful hit knocks loot out of him, ending in an A- or S-tier item on the final hit (same source).
- *Ideas worth knowing:* (1) A boss who has been *taking things from you all run* turns grievance into motivation; the payoff is getting your stuff back mid-fight. (2) Breakable weapon parts that downgrade the boss's moveset give skilled players a visible, tactical way to make the fight easier.

**The Old King.** An optional boss in the secret Abbey of the True Gun with 3,333.2 HP; he is a harder remix of the first-floor Bullet King, following the player while firing more complex volleys, including jammed-bullet rings that deal a full heart of damage (https://enterthegungeon.fandom.com/wiki/Old_King). The wiki notes he is a reference to Old King Doran of *Demon's Souls* and suggests he was the previous Lead Lord (same source). Like the Bullet King, he has a Chancellor beside him; a *Casey* bat hit that sends the Chancellor's body into the King deals 1000 × 1.666… × 2 = 3,333.3 damage, a hair over his health — an Easter-egg one-shot (same source).
- *Ideas worth knowing:* (1) A secret boss as an "elder version" of the first boss re-uses the player's learned reads and then breaks them. (2) Hidden one-shot interactions reward curiosity.

**The Advanced Dragun.** The High Dragun is the final boss of the Forge (phase 1 2,767.8 HP; a phase 2 where he is invulnerable and fills the room while his heart is briefly exposed between waves) (https://enterthegungeon.fandom.com/wiki/High_Dragun). Since the Advanced Gungeons & Draguns update, the player can unlock a third phase: Rat keys (up to six per Rat fight, dropped only in the Punch-Out phase) open a secret room with a baby serpent; fed four items, it follows the player and blocks bullets — then abandons the player at the Dragun, who absorbs it and becomes the gold-plated Advanced Dragun (5,200 HP) with a new, harder moveset, including fireballs that create zones where the player cannot shoot and an eye on the floor that dims the room (same source).
- *Ideas worth knowing:* (1) The *player* opts into the harder boss through a chain of optional deeds across the run — difficulty as a deliberate sacrifice. (2) The companion the player raised betrays them and becomes the boss's power-up: a strong narrative beat that costs almost no new art.

### 2.2 Nuclear Throne

**The Nuclear Throne and Throne II.** The Nuclear Throne is the final boss of the Palace (7-3), 1,500 HP; it begins as a static cannon then grows legs and walks, cycling beam, orb and tri-shot phases; standing on the red carpet baits its beam; and as it walks it smashes Throne Statues which each release a Guardian (https://nuclear-throne.fandom.com/wiki/Nuclear_Throne). Destroying all four Large Generators (230 HP each) during the fight removes half its remaining HP and guarantees a Loop Portal (same source). Each loop adds projectiles (orbs split into one more row; tri-shot gains a bullet per row; statues release an extra Guardian) (same source). Throne II (880 HP) is the "final form", fought at the Campfire on a loop: it appears after the last I.D.P.D. unit there dies, spawning on that corpse — "Due to this, Throne II can kill you instantly while spawning in" (https://nuclear-throne.fandom.com/wiki/Throne_II). It circles the arena, switching direction, with orb-burst and static star phases, and each loop adds an orb and a split-line (same source).
- *Ideas worth knowing:* (1) Environmental pieces in the arena (generators) give a strategic alternative to raw DPS, and reward a "loop" route — a direct template for "destroy the braziers to weaken the boss and open the endless mode". (2) Loop scaling by adding *more of the same pattern* (one more row, one more bullet) is cheap, legible escalation for an endless phase. (3) Throne II's spawn-on-corpse kill is a cautionary tale: never spawn a boss onto the player without a telegraph.

**Big Fish — correcting the brief.** Big Fish is not a boss players fight. The wiki's Oasis page says it is an *unused* boss, "fully sprited and exists in the game files", that was meant to replace the Big Bandit when the Oasis secret was triggered, "but ultimately was not added to the game" (https://nuclear-throne.fandom.com/wiki/Oasis). The actual Oasis route: open the Desert's chests and radiation canister without killing many enemies, then kill the Big Bandit within 10 seconds to open the Oasis portal; the Oasis jumps you to 3-3 without the difficulty catching up (same source).
- *Idea worth knowing:* A *timed* kill of a mini-boss as the key to a secret route (kill it fast and the world changes) is a clean, skill-based branch.

### 2.3 The Binding of Isaac

**Mom.** The original final boss, on the second floor of Chapter 3; she is never fully on-screen — she stomps from above (always first in the centre, telegraphed by a growing shadow and grunts), reaches out of the four boss doors, peers in with an eye, and spawns monsters through the doors, up to 3 alive at once (https://bindingofisaacrebirth.fandom.com/wiki/Mom). Beating her for the first time unlocks the epilogue and Chapter 4 (same source). In Repentance a stronger Mom on the alternate path triple-stomps with explosions (same source). Crucially, her room opens a **gateway to the Boss Rush only if the player reaches it under 20:00** (25:00 on the alternate path) (same source).
- *Ideas worth knowing:* (1) A boss that is "too big to see" and attacks from the arena's edges and doors makes the room itself the boss. (2) Speed-gated bonus content: beat the boss under a time and an extra challenge opens — directly portable to a 30:00 ember boss with a "kill it by 32:00 for a bonus encounter" hook.

**It Lives.** In Chapter 4, Mom's Heart is "permanently replaced" by It Lives after the player has killed Mom's Heart 11 times; It Lives behaves the same but summons different and harder minions in each of its three phases and finishes with four-way brimstone lasers that spin (https://bindingofisaacrebirth.fandom.com/wiki/It_Lives). It also opens the gateway to Blue Womb/??? when reached in under 30:00 (same source).
- *Idea worth knowing:* A boss that *upgrades itself permanently* after N victories turns mastery into progression and keeps a familiar final boss from going stale.

**Mother.** The Repentance boss of the Corpse (4,222 HP). The room appears empty but for a hole; dropping in starts the fight (https://bindingofisaacrebirth.fandom.com/wiki/Mother). In phase 1 she clings to the top wall and her face and both hands are separate damage targets; she bats a growing Fistula around the room which melee attacks can reflect — six reflects stun and damage her (same source). She also withdraws, invulnerable, and summons a horde of 34 Dead Isaacs that march at her; survivors are swiped and their corpses flung at the player (same source). At 2,222 HP lost she fires a laser that blows out the lower wall, doubling the arena, and the fight changes shape (same source).
- *Ideas worth knowing:* (1) A *horde phase inside a boss fight* — the boss pauses and the player clears adds — is exactly the survivors-genre beat to borrow. (2) Doubling the arena mid-fight is a strong phase marker.

**Dogma.** The first boss of Home, 2,400 HP. Phase 1: Dogma is invulnerable and only the television in the centre takes damage; Dogma creeps towards the player trailing static creep (https://bindingofisaacrebirth.fandom.com/wiki/Dogma). Phase 2: the TV breaks, Dogma rises as a seraph and becomes vulnerable, with telegraphed light-ray patterns and Ophanim-wheel charges; as it weakens it flickers into other forms (purely visual) (same source). Killing it hands the player the Dogma item and plays the cutscene that introduces the Beast (same source).
- *Idea worth knowing:* "Hit the object, not the boss" — the damage target is a separate prop the boss protects.

**The Beast.** The final boss of the whole game (10,000 HP). Dogma's defeat grants *flight*, a one-off shield and a heal, changing the player's movement rules for the finale (https://bindingofisaacrebirth.fandom.com/wiki/The_Beast). The fight opens with four Ultra Harbingers (Famine, Pestilence, War, Death) fought one at a time while the Beast waits in the background; then a 2D side-scrolling arena where her charges scroll the screen and bring stalactites at the player, followed by fire rings with one gap, a colossal beam, and (at 6,660 HP and roughly 33%) new phases; her body visibly decays — horns crack, eyes and teeth are lost — at about 2,000, 1,000 and 500 HP (same source).
- *Ideas worth knowing:* (1) A gauntlet of previous bosses' "ultra" forms as the opening act of the final boss. (2) Visible damage states as a health bar the player reads from the art. (3) Granting a new movement ability for the finale only.

### 2.4 Hyper Light Breaker — Crowns on an open map

**Correcting the brief.** On the sources I could check, the Crowns do not roam. Each of the five Crowns lives in one biome of the open, procedurally generated Overgrowth, inside an enclosure behind a Crown Gate (https://hyperlightbreaker.fandom.com/wiki/Crowns). The gates open with Prisms (formerly "keys") dropped by Elite mini-bosses, and "some Crown Gates require more Prisms than others" (https://hyperlightbreaker.fandom.com/wiki/Prisms). What *does* roam are Assassins — hunters such as the "Infiltrator, a hard-as-nails wanderer who shoots first and asks questions later" (Double Down patch notes, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1808601382418696).

**The loop around the bosses.** The launch guide describes the run as: explore the Overgrowth, get Prisms, unlock Crown Gates, extract when things get tough, fight Crowns, end the Cycle, repeat (https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1788311419183497). A "Death Meter" filled as players killed, looted and found secrets, triggering events; and "After you defeat a Crown, the meter will fill up all the way, so we recommend evacuating/extracting out of the Overgrowth before it's too late" (same source). The Buried Below update (April 2025) later removed the danger meter and replaced it with a visible World Rank: "As World Rank increases, enemies and bosses grow in power - but so does the available loot", and moved the game to one life per Cycle (https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1797820624627362). Each Crown's backstory is told through collectable "memory" fragments on the map, shown as wordless manga-like panels (launch guide, above).

**Notable Crowns.** At early-access launch there were two Crowns, Dro and Exus (https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1788311419021302). Buried Below added the Maw, "our largest Crown yet", who "lurks deep below the surface" of the Jeweled Spire with self-destructing Hatchling adds (Buried Below notes, above). Double Down (August 2025) added the Twins, Bersia and Vesia, fought together (https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1808601382418696); PC Gamer's headline calls it a duo "that's 'technically 4 bosses' depending on what order you beat them in" (https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/PC%20Gamer/1808601382419177; the article body was not retrievable, so the mechanism is not confirmed here). Reception: the game sat at 63% "Mixed" on Steam a month into early access, with Heart Machine admitting a "teamwide oops, guess we made it too hard moment" (PC Gamer via Steam news, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/PC%20Gamer/1791580425997466); development later ended before 1.0 amid layoffs (PCGamesN via Steam news, https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/PCGamesN/1813041031209285).

- *Ideas worth knowing:* (1) Killing a boss as the *trigger* for the world turning hostile ("the meter fills all the way") — a direct parallel to Survivor Unchained's endless phase after the 30:00 win. (2) Boss access gated by elites spread across the map, so the boss is the destination of the run rather than a timer. (3) A duo boss whose second half changes based on which twin dies first multiplies content cheaply.

### 2.5 Rogue Legacy 2 — Prime bosses

Every main boss "has a far more difficult Prime version which can be fought in New Game Plus", dropping more resources and unlocking more lore (https://rogue-legacy-2.fandom.com/wiki/Bosses). Each Prime is opted into as a *Burden* when starting a new Thread (NG+), one per boss, unlocking from NG+1 (Lamech) to NG+7 (Cain); the burden text frames each as a rift to the boss's past — "Fight Lamech prior to being wounded during the rebellion", "Fight Naamah before the famine", "Fight Enoch before he succumbs to the poison", "Fight Tubal before the madness sinks in", "Fight Cain before the guilt" (https://rogue-legacy-2.fandom.com/wiki/New_Game_Plus). Some are "harder variations of his usual attacks" (Lamech), others add new set pieces (Irad, Tubal) (same source). The True Ending requires enabling all Prime Boss Burdens on a Thread of at least NG+3 (same source). Separately, the Scars of Erebus are optional remix challenges: two Lamechs at once, Lamech and Naamah together, True Enoch in a small arena, or waves of automatons followed by four automaton mini-bosses — all at a fixed level with no equipment (https://rogue-legacy-2.fandom.com/wiki/Scars_of_Erebus). The base bosses also have two-stage reveals: after Enoch dies "a fake chest will appear, which is the next phase of the boss" (https://rogue-legacy-2.fandom.com/wiki/Estuary_Enoch), and reading a hidden memory grants +15% permanent damage against him (same source).

- *Ideas worth knowing:* (1) The harder remix is *the same boss in its prime*: the difficulty bump is justified by story and pays out new lore, not just bigger numbers. (2) Remixes are an opt-in burden alongside other run modifiers, letting players tune their own challenge. (3) Lore-for-damage (read the memory, hit harder) ties exploration to the boss fight — a natural day-to-night bridge for Survivor Unchained.

### 2.6 Wizard of Legend — the Council of Magic

The Chaos Trials are three randomly ordered sections, each themed on one of three Council members drawn from five elements (Water, Fire, Wind, Lightning, Earth); each section has three stages, the first two with a guaranteed relic shop, arcana shop, mystery room and mini-boss, the third a Council boss (https://wizardoflegend.fandom.com/wiki/Chaos_Trials). Later sections make bosses perform more attacks, gain new ones, have shorter vulnerability windows and move faster (same source). Concretely, "the council members use three of the above attacks when they are the first council member. Each council member adds one more attack before their dance. The second uses four, the third uses five, and Master Sura uses six"; Zeal's Fire Mines appear only as boss 2 or above and Rushing Brush Fire only as boss 3 (https://wizardoflegend.fandom.com/wiki/Flame_Empress_Zeal). Most Council attacks "are based on existing Arcana, but bigger and with added effects" — the bosses use the player's own spell list (https://wizardoflegend.fandom.com/wiki/Council_of_Magic). Master Sura, always fought fourth, starts stationary behind a chaos barrier, then in phase 2 dashes around performing one attack of each element "ending in an attack from another council member" before a chaos signature (https://wizardoflegend.fandom.com/wiki/Master_Sura). The lightning twins Juno and Suman are a combo duo whose attacks are two-part (Suman lays metal plates or balls; Juno strikes them) (https://wizardoflegend.fandom.com/wiki/Juno_and_Suman).

- *Ideas worth knowing:* (1) **Position-scaled bosses**: the same boss adds attacks depending on whether it is fought first, second or third, so random ordering never breaks the difficulty curve. (2) Bosses wield the player's own spells, amplified — a strong fit for Survivor Unchained's drafted weapons (a boss that fires a corrupted version of the weapons you drafted). (3) A final boss who quotes every earlier boss.

## Lessons for Survivor Unchained

1. **"Can spawn from X, guaranteed by Y."** The Simulacrum's boss windows (Omniphobia possible from wave 10, guaranteed by 15) give tension without total predictability. For a 30:00 ember arena, consider heralds or mini-bosses that may appear in a window (say 20:00–25:00) and are guaranteed by its end, with the great boss fixed at 30:00.
2. **The boss should inherit the run's choices.** Ultimatum carries every accepted modifier into the Trialmaster fight; Infernal Hordes' offers stack across waves. If the ember arena offers curse-for-power bargains, let them visibly shape the 30:00 boss (more adds, hazards moved to the centre, shorter windows).
3. **Make the bargain two-sided and avoid a dominant pick.** D4 players simply "go for Hellborne every time"; Grim Dawn's mutators are always "stronger in one way, weaker in another". Balance offers so none is an automatic choice.
4. **Soften all-or-nothing.** PoE2 reworked Trial of Chaos (exit any time, retries) and softened Honour; Blight banks a lane's chest even if the pump later falls; losing Gungeon's Punch-Out phase against the Rat still lets you keep the drops. Bank the first 15 minutes' rewards so that a death to the 30:00 boss is not a total loss.
5. **Give the boss's arrival a grace beat and a telegraph.** GGG added a grace period on teleport into the Trialmaster's arena; Blizzard added audio cues for wave start and end; Nuclear Throne's Throne II can kill the player as it spawns. Telegraph the 30:00 boss audibly and visually, and never spawn it on the player.
6. **Put a horde phase inside the boss.** Isaac's Mother withdraws and sends 34 Dead Isaacs; Gungeon's Rat summons adds; Crucible stacks multiple bosses late on. A survivors boss should periodically become a horde-clearing problem, not just a bullet-pattern problem.
7. **Use the arena as the alternative to DPS.** Nuclear Throne's generators (destroy all four: half HP gone and the loop portal guaranteed) and Isaac's Dogma TV ("hit the object, not the boss") suggest braziers or ember-wells that weaken the boss and decide whether the endless phase opens.
8. **Killing the boss should change the world.** Hyper Light Breaker filled its danger meter to maximum the moment a Crown died. Survivor Unchained's endless post-win phase can be framed the same way: the boss's death enrages the night until dawn.
9. **Remix bosses with story, not just numbers.** Rogue Legacy 2's Primes are the same bosses "before the famine" or "before the guilt"; Gungeon's Old King is the elder Bullet King; Isaac's It Lives replaces Mom's Heart after 11 kills. A night boss could return in an earlier, unbroken form after it has been beaten by day.
10. **Opt-in difficulty via deeds.** The Advanced Dragun only appears if the player raised the serpent; Rogue Legacy 2's Primes are chosen burdens; Isaac's Boss Rush opens only if Mom dies before 20:00. Hidden or optional conditions in the arena (e.g. kill the 30:00 boss before 32:00) can unlock a harder variant or a bonus fight.
11. **Scale by position when order is random.** Wizard of Legend adds one attack per boss slot. If blessings or biomes vary per night, the 30:00 boss should scale by how far into the campaign or ember-heat the player is, not by fixed identity.
12. **Let bosses use the player's kit.** Wizard of Legend's Council casts amplified versions of the player's arcana; a boss that fires corrupted versions of the drafted weapons reads instantly and makes each run's boss different.
13. **Log telemetry per minute.** GGG's per-wave completion data pinpointed the walls at waves 15, 18 and 20. Record survival rate by minute and by boss for the ember arena from the first playtest.
