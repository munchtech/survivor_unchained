## Goal
We're trialling a signature look: painterly realism. Forms stay real. Surfaces read as painted: simplified texture detail, visible but controlled brushwork at ONE consistent screen scale, fewer micro-details, no outlines, no cel look. Add a post-filter that does the "surfaces painted" half in engine, at the play camera (high, looking down at the town or the ford) and at the camp close-up. **The heroine is never filtered** (her face, hair, skin and outfit are our biggest investment): mask her out exactly.

Round 1's lesson: a naive screen filter "reads as filtered, not painted" (smears, blotches, oil-paint blobs). So aim for: an anisotropic-Kuwahara-type, structure-aligned simplification at a small radius (flat paint patches that follow the forms and keep edges), plus a subtle stroke texture along the local flow (strokes about 2-4 px wide and 8-12 px long at 1920x1080), at partial strength. A numpy prototype of the flow field and the LIC stroke texture is `brush()` in `tools/scratch/art_style/paintprep_r1.py` (structure tensor, flow LIC, LIC-noise strokes): copy its ideas, not its speed.

## Requirements

3. **Her mask, exact:** her pixels must come out unfiltered. My suggestion: a half- or full-res mask `SubViewport` sharing the world, its camera synced each frame to the main camera (transform, FOV/size, near/far, offsets), `cull_mask` set to one spare visual layer that only her meshes (body, hair, eyes, outfit, armour, shield and weapon too) carry in addition to their normal layers, `debug_draw` unshaded, `transparent_bg`: its alpha is her coverage.
