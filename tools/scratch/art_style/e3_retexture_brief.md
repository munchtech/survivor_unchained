## What the target shows (the lead's reading)
- **Setts:** stones of varied size and shape (a few big flat flags, mostly mid stones, small fill stones), each one a flat painted tone: warm grey-ochre in the sun, slightly lighter on the top and sun-side rim, with a painted light edge. **Joints filled with opaque, matte ochre-brown mud, not dark lines,** and uneven in width; moss green at edges and near the well; a lighter, polished wear lane down the street. Brushwork in the albedo at one scale (about 1.5-2 cm wide in the world: 4-5 px wide and 12-20 px long at the tile's 256 px/m), following each stone's long axis. Fewer micro-details: no grain noise; value shapes over texture. Env lead 8's notes agree: vary the cell sizes and the joints, make the mud opaque and matte over the buried edges, carry the wear lane in the albedo.
- **Well:** a rough stone ring with light painted tops and dark gaps; warm terracotta round tiles on the roof (the kit's own `RoundTiles`); warm painted wood.
- **Stall:** sun-faded painted canvas stripes with soft folds and a darker valance shadow; warm painted wood.
- **Folk:** warm, varied clothes (ochre, madder red, moss, indigo, undyed linen), never grey.

## The jobs (in this order; report after each)

Add a Godot flag `--kit-paint DIR`: `KitMaterials.Get` in `godot/src/World/Landmarks.cs` swaps a material's albedo when DIR holds a file of that texture's name.
